# pi² for iPhone: the contract

This is the contract for epic [#620](https://github.com/KellamA/pi2/issues/620), pi² for iPhone: how a phone is paired with a Mac, how the engine knows a paired phone speaks for the person, how the two reach each other on any two networks, what a notification carries, what moves into a package both apps build on, and how all of it is tested with no phone in hand. Every later issue of the epic is built against it, several at once, by agents who read this page and not the conversation in which it was made. Where this page and an issue's text differ, this page has the last word on names and shapes; [section 12](#12-the-later-issues) says who owns each piece that several issues share.

The design it builds is [design/iphone.md](design/iphone.md), with its drawings in [design/iphone-drafts.html](design/iphone-drafts.html). This page does not repeat why the phone exists; it says exactly what. Where the design left something open, this page decides it, and [section 13](#13-decisions-this-contract-made) lists each decision. What only the owner can decide is in [section 2](#2-the-decisions-and-where-each-lives) (the three decisions asked, one answered) and [section 14](#14-questions-for-the-owner) (the rest). Nothing in the epic starts until the owner has agreed this page and a second agent has read it for security.

**After the security reading.** A second agent read this page for security (#635, step P1). Its six must-fix findings, eleven should-fix and nine notes are answered in place, in #651; [section 13](#13-decisions-this-contract-made) (24 to 32) lists what changed and where a finding was refined, and [section 14](#14-questions-for-the-owner) has the questions as the reading left them, all answered yes by the owner on 2026-11-21. The owner answered M3 while this was written: **Face ID to open the app, nothing after** ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)), which is not the reading's recommendation; 4.2, 4.3, section 7 and section 11 are written for it. No section was renumbered; 4.5 and 12.3 are new, and so are P0 and P37.

**How to read it.** Read [section 1](#1-words), [section 2](#2-the-decisions-and-where-each-lives), [section 4](#4-who-is-speaking), [5.1](#51-the-shape-one-channel-two-paths) and [5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them) whatever your issue, then [12.1](#121-who-owns-what-several-issues-share) and the sections your issue's row in [12.2](#122-each-issue) names. Types are written as TypeScript, as `engine/src/state.ts` writes them; times are milliseconds since 1970. Apple's and Cloudflare's limits and prices are cited in the notes at the end, from their own documentation; a number this page gives without a note is this page's own choice.

## 1. Words

- **The phone**: pi² for iPhone, the app. What is said of it holds for any paired device; an iPad later is one more device.
- **The Mac**: the Mac whose engine the phone is paired with. One phone may later be paired with several Macs; this contract builds one, and keeps a pairing's id in everything so that more can be added.
- **The app**: the Mac app, as everywhere in `docs/`.
- **pi² Remote**: a helper program inside the app's bundle that holds the Mac's ends of both paths and its keys ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)). The engine starts it and is its judge.
- **The relay**: a small service pi² runs, on Cloudflare ([5.3](#53-the-relay)), that passes sealed bytes between a Mac and its paired phones, keeps a small mailbox of sealed items for an end that is away, and sends pushes. It can read none of what it passes.
- **Pairing**: at the relay, a Mac and its paired devices, under a random id. In the engine, the record of [3.2](#32-the-keys).
- **Device**: a paired phone, as the engine records it.
- **Channel**: a Noise session between the phone and pi² Remote ([5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise)), over either path.
- **Path**: what carries a channel: `relay` (the main path, on any two networks), `home` (TCP on the same network, found by Bonjour) or `direct` (TCP to another address of the Mac's, such as a private network's).
- **Device session** (also *device connection*): one attached device's place in the engine, carried on the control connection between the engine and the pi² Remote it started, after the device proved itself to the engine ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection), [4.1](#41-from-the-app-or-from-a-paired-device)). The engine treats it as a connection of its own.
- **Open session**: the time between Face ID on opening pi² on the phone and the end [4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice) gives it. Everything a phone may send is sent in an open session, and nothing is asked again inside it.
- **Presence signature**: a signature by a key the phone can use only under the Face ID that opened the session ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)). It is on every attach, every resume and every item for the mailbox.
- **Sealed item**: a message encrypted for one reader with HPKE and signed by its writer, which can wait in the relay's mailbox ([5.3](#53-the-relay)).
- **Outbox**: what the phone has written and not yet seen taken by the Mac.

## 3. Pairing

### 3.1 What the Mac shows and the phone scans

**Settings › Devices › Pair an iPhone** asks the engine for a pairing (`devices.pairStart`, in `FOR_THE_PERSON_SETTINGS` like every `devices.*` request but `devices.self` and `devices.forgetSelf`, [4.1](#41-from-the-app-or-from-a-paired-device)). The engine has pi² Remote register the pairing with the relay if it has none ([5.3](#53-the-relay)) and open a **join window** there for five minutes, and the app draws the payload as a QR code. The payload, and `s` in it, are in the answer to `devices.pairStart` on the app's own connection and nowhere else: no event carries them, no other connection or device is sent them, and no log holds them. The payload is a text in the form of a URL:

```
pi2://pair?v=2&p=<pairing>&n=<Mac's name>&c=<Mac's channel key>&i=<Mac's identity key>&r=<Mac's record key>&s=<secret>&u=<relay>&a=<address:port,…>&e=<expiry>
```

A release build of the phone reads it only from its own camera scanner. It does not register `pi2:` as a URL scheme and takes a pairing from no link, message, web page or other app: a link must never be able to pair a person's phone with a stranger's Mac, which would then receive what they type, dictate and share. Debug builds take it from `xcrun simctl openurl` for testing ([10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)).

- `p`: the pairing's id at the relay, 16 random bytes the relay chose, base64url.
- `c`: the Mac's channel key, a Curve25519 public key, base64url. The phone accepts a channel only with the holder of this key ([5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise)).
- `i`: the Mac's identity key, a P-256 public key (SPKI DER, base64url), which signs the Mac's side of every connection and everything the Mac seals ([3.2](#32-the-keys)).
- `r`: the Mac's record key, a P-256 public key (SPKI DER, base64url), to which sealed items for the Mac are sealed.
- `s`: 32 random bytes, made for this pairing alone and kept only in the engine's memory and pi² Remote's until the window closes. It is the pre-shared key of the pairing handshake, and the relay is given only a hash of a value made from it ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)).
- `u`: the relay's address, such as `wss://relay.example.dev/v1`.
- `a`: the Mac's addresses on its network now, for the `home` path.
- `e`: seconds since 1970. The window is open for five minutes, for **one claim**: it closes after a phone pairs, after a second claim on either path ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)), after three wrong tries (a wrong token at the relay, or a wrong pre-shared key on the `home` path), when the sheet closes, or when it expires.

The QR code is shown only in the app's sheet, never written to disk or a log. The sheet asks macOS to keep it out of screen captures and screen sharing (`NSWindow.sharingType = .none`); nothing relies on that, since an agent's commands can take pictures of the screen and computer use sees it ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange) is built for an attacker who has read the code). It holds no account, password or long-lived secret: `s` is good once, for five minutes, and pairs only with a genuine pi² for iPhone that the Mac checks itself, and only when the person types on the Mac the six digits that phone shows.

### 3.2 The keys

**On the phone**, made at pairing. The Secure Enclave holds only P-256 keys and cannot import one;[^se] Noise's standard key agreement is Curve25519,[^noise] which the Secure Enclave cannot hold. So the key that proves the device is in the Secure Enclave, and the channel key, which is Curve25519, is bound to it by a signature at every connection ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)).

| Key | What it does | Where and how it is kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Device key** (P-256, signing) | Proves the device to the relay and, over the channel's handshake hash, to the Mac; signs every sealed item the phone writes | Secure Enclave, `kSecAttrAccessibleWhenPasscodeSetThisDeviceOnly` with `privateKeyUsage`: usable only while the phone is unlocked, never backed up or moved, deleted if the passcode is turned off.[^passcode] |
| **Presence key** (P-256, signing) | Signs every attach, every resume and every item for the mailbox, under the Face ID that opened the session ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)) | Secure Enclave, as the device key, plus `biometryCurrentSet` and no passcode fallback: it can be used only under an authentication context that Face ID itself evaluated, and it is invalidated if Face ID is enrolled again.[^biocurrent] **Without Face ID, the phone cannot open a session**: it shows its notifications and nothing else. |
| **Channel key** (Curve25519) | The phone's static key in Noise ([5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise)) | Keychain, `WhenPasscodeSetThisDeviceOnly`: as the device key, but not in the Secure Enclave |
| **Record key** (P-256, key agreement) | Opens what is sealed to the phone: a push's content and the mailbox's items, in the app and in its notification service extension | Keychain, `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`, in an access group the extension shares, so a notification that arrives while the phone is locked can be opened; in the Secure Enclave if CryptoKit's HPKE takes a Secure Enclave key on the iOS version P14 targets, which P6 finds out: as far as compiling shows, it does, from iOS 17, as a reader's key and a writer's ([remotekit.md](remotekit.md#sealed-items)), though no Secure Enclave key has yet been made and used; whether such a key opens items in the extension while the phone is locked is for P12 and P21 to try |
| **App Attest keys** (two) | Prove, at pairing, to the relay and to the Mac, that the phone runs a genuine pi² for iPhone signed by pi²'s team, and that this device key is that copy's ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)).[^attest] App Attest attests a key only once (`DCError.invalidKey` after), so the phone has two: one attested to the relay at `join`, one to the Mac in `pair`. A join that is dropped before it is answered costs the relay's key, so each join attempt makes a new one | Made and held by `DCAppAttestService`, in the Secure Enclave |
| **Cache key** (symmetric) | Encrypts the phone's cache | Keychain, `WhenPasscodeSetThisDeviceOnly` with `biometryCurrentSet`, read under the session's authentication context, so the cache opens only in an open session |
| **What the phone keeps of the Mac** | The pairing's id, the Mac's name, channel key, identity key, record key, the relay's address, the Mac's last addresses | Keychain, `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`, the same access group |

The presence key is made at step 4 of pairing, which needs Face ID enrolled but asks for no Face ID; Face ID is first asked when the first session opens, at the end of pairing. Phones with Touch ID in place of Face ID are as safe, since `biometryCurrentSet` covers both; this page says "Face ID" for either ([section 14](#14-questions-for-the-owner), question 8).

A new presence key (Face ID enrolled again, or Face ID turned on later) is sent to the Mac and used only once the person accepts it on the Mac's Devices page (`devices.acceptPresenceKey`, the app's alone), which reads: *Face ID was set up again on Alex's iPhone. Accept only if you did it yourself.* The Mac also posts a notification when one waits ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)). Otherwise whoever knows the passcode could enrol a face and gain Face ID's answers.

The first launch after the app is installed deletes any pi² Keychain items it finds: Keychain items may outlive deleting the app, and a reinstall must not find old keys.

**On the Mac**, made by pi² Remote when remote access is first turned on. Those that can be are in the Mac's Secure Enclave (Apple silicon, or an Intel Mac with a T2 chip); the rest, and all of them on a Mac without one, are in the data-protection Keychain in an access group only pi²'s signed code has, so `/usr/bin/security` and an agent's commands cannot read them ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them) says what a second copy of pi²'s own code could do, and how that is stopped):

| Key | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity key** (P-256, signing) | The Mac's identity: signs the handshake hash at every connection, as the device key does on the phone ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)), and every sealed item, state, push content and revocation notice the Mac writes ([5.3](#53-the-relay)); its public half is `i` | Secure Enclave where there is one |
| **Channel key** (Curve25519) | The Mac's static key in Noise; its public half is `c` | Keychain |
| **Record key** (P-256, key agreement) | Opens items sealed to the Mac; seals items and push contents for a device | Secure Enclave if CryptoKit's HPKE takes one, which P6 found it does from macOS 14 as far as compiling shows (no Secure Enclave key was made; P7 tries one), else Keychain |
| **Relay key** (P-256, signing) | Proves to the relay that this Mac owns the pairing | Secure Enclave where there is one |
| **The paired devices' public keys** | pi² Remote's own list, which it attaches no device outside of ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)) | Keychain |

A copy of the Keychain's keys therefore cannot pose as the Mac anywhere a phone checks the identity key. The Devices page says, on a Mac without a Secure Enclave, that its keys are in the Keychain only.

A development build or throwaway copy, which is signed ad hoc and has no access group, keeps them in a file in its own data folder, readable only by the person, and talks only to a relay on this Mac ([10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)); a release build never does either.

**The Mac's record of a device**, kept by the engine:

```ts
type Device = {
	id: string; // "dev_" and 16 random bytes, base64url; also the device's slot at the relay
	name: string; // as the phone gave it ("Alex's iPhone"); the person can rename it on the Mac
	model: string; // "iPhone 17 Pro"
	system: string; // "iOS 27.1", updated at each connection
	deviceKey: string; // public halves, SPKI DER or raw, base64
	presenceKey: string | null;
	pendingPresenceKey: string | null; // a new one, until the person accepts it on the Mac
	channelKey: string;
	recordKey: string;
	attestKeyId: string; // the App Attest key the Mac checked at pairing
	build: string; // the app's build, updated at each connection (S7's minimum)
	suspendedAt: number | null; // suspended by the Mac after repeated failures (4.5), until the person resumes it
	pairedAt: number;
	lastSeenAt: number | null;
	lastPath: "relay" | "home" | "direct" | "mailbox" | null;
	revokedAt: number | null; // kept 90 days for the log, then removed
};

type Devices = {
	// the document `pi2.devices`
	pairing: string | null; // the pairing's id at the relay
	relay: string; // the relay's address; the release build's own unless the person changed it
	macName: string;
	remote: { on: boolean; relay: boolean; home: boolean; direct: boolean; preview: boolean }; // relay: false is "home and private networks only" (6.5); direct: S6's any-address switch
	macKeys: { identity: string; channel: string; record: string; relay: string; secureEnclave: boolean } | null; // public halves, as pi² Remote reported them
	devices: Device[];
};
```

### 3.3 The pairing exchange

The exchange is built for an attacker who has read the QR code: someone looking at the screen, a screen share or recording, or an agent on the Mac, whose commands can take pictures of the screen. Such an attacker can complete the handshake; it cannot get the person's phone to show its digits, and it cannot pass App Attest with a key of its own.

1. The phone scans the code, and at once tries both paths: the relay at `u`, and `home` (Bonjour `_pi2._tcp`, recognised as in [5.4](#54-direct-at-home), and the addresses in `a`). Pairing works with the phone on any network.
2. **On the relay path**, the phone first joins, with the device key it made on scanning the code: it sends the relay `join { pairing, deviceKey, attestation, keyId, token }`, where `attestation` is App Attest's attestation of a new key of its own, made for this join ([3.2](#32-the-keys)), with the relay's challenge and the device key as client data (its client data hash is SHA-256 of the 32-byte challenge followed by the device key's SPKI DER, so the attested copy of the app vouches for this device key),[^attest] and `token` is HMAC-SHA256 keyed with `s` of the text `pi2-join` and the pairing's id. The relay checks the window is open, the token against the hash the Mac gave it, and the attestation against Apple's root and pi²'s app id; it gives the phone a slot and connects it to the Mac's socket. A wrong token, a refused attestation, or an App Attest key already tied to another pairing or device counts against the window's three tries ([relay/README.md](../relay/README.md#what-it-decides)). This check keeps the relay to genuine copies of the app ([6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost)); the Mac does not take the relay's word for it (step 5). On the `home` path nothing is asked of the relay.
3. Over the first path to connect, the phone runs **Noise_IKpsk2_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256**:[^noise] it knows the Mac's static key `c`, the pre-shared key is `s`, and the prologue is `pi2-pair-v2` and the pairing's id. Only the holder of the Mac's channel key and of `s` can complete it. On the `home` path a handshake that fails on the pre-shared key counts against the window's three tries. No handshake message carries a payload ([5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise)).
4. The Mac's first transport message is `{"challenge":<32 bytes>,"mac":{"signed":…,"signature":…}}`, where `signed` is `{"v":2,"kind":"mac","pairing":…,"binding":<the handshake hash>}` signed by the Mac's identity key, which the phone checks against `i`. The phone then makes its other keys (the device key it made on scanning the code) and answers `{"pair":{"v":2,"device":{…},"attestation":…,"keyId":…,"signed":…,"signature":…}}`: `device` holds the name, model, system, build and the four public keys (device, presence, channel, record); `attestation` is the attestation of a second new App Attest key, not the relay's ([3.2](#32-the-keys)), whose client data hash is SHA-256 of the challenge, the device key's public half and the handshake hash, so the attested copy of the app vouches for this very device key on this very channel; `signed` is the JSON text `{"v":2,"kind":"pair","pairing":…,"binding":<the handshake hash>}` and `signature` the device key's over it (Noise's channel binding is the handshake hash[^noise]).
5. pi² Remote passes it to the engine (`remote.pairClaim`, with the handshake hash and the challenge). **The engine checks the claim itself, on either path**: the window is open and has had no other claim; the signature; the attestation, against Apple's App Attest root and pi²'s team and app id, with the client data hash above (`engine/src/appattest.ts`, the same code the relay runs, P37); and that fewer than five devices are paired. An attestation can be checked only while its leaf certificate is valid, which Apple makes from about a day before it is made until two days after, so the engine and the relay check it as it arrives, at their own time; a clock more than a day off refuses it, and the refusal says which certificate and which way. pi² for iPhone is not offered on Macs (App Store Connect's and TestFlight's "available on Mac" off), and Apple turns App Attest off for an iOS app running on a Mac, so Apple's macOS-only step (the aclBlob, extension 1.2.840.113635.100.8.6) is not made; if either changes, that step is added first. A build without App Attest (the simulator, [10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)) is taken only by an engine that is not a release build, in a throwaway copy.
6. **One claim per window.** A second claim, on either path, closes the window and refuses both, and the sheet says: *Another device tried to use this code. Start again, and don't show the code on a shared or recorded screen.*
7. The phone shows six digits worked out from `s` and the handshake hash (HMAC-SHA256 with the label `pi2-digits`, the first 32 bits modulo a million), with *Type these digits on your Mac*. **The person types them on the Mac**, in the sheet, which sends `devices.pairConfirm { digits }` (the app's alone). The engine works out the same digits for the claim and pairs only if they match; there is no button that accepts a claim as such. Three wrong entries close the window. A claimer who does not hold the person's phone cannot make the digits match but by a one-in-a-million guess.
8. The engine stores the device and gives pi² Remote its keys for its own list ([3.2](#32-the-keys)); pi² Remote tells the relay to add the device's slot with its device key and closes the window; the phone is answered `{"paired":{"device":…,"pairing":…,"name":…,"addresses":[…]}}`. The Mac posts a notification that a device was paired ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)).
9. The phone keeps what [3.2](#32-the-keys) says, asks for notifications and then for Face ID (each with a line on what it is for), sends the relay its push token ([7](#7-notifications)), and connects as any paired phone does.

The `devices` event, which carries claims, their digits' state and the devices' keys, is sent only to connections the engine takes for the app ([4.1](#41-from-the-app-or-from-a-paired-device)), never to other connections and never to a device ([5.7](#57-what-is-never-sent-to-a-phone)).

**Pairing again.** A phone that is paired and scans another code asks *Replace your pairing with ‹Mac›?*, and on yes unpairs itself first ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)); a phone pairs with one Mac at a time. On the Mac, a claim whose model and name match a paired device is shown with **Replace Alex's iPhone**: pairing it revokes the old record in the same commit.

Pairing is only ever started and confirmed on the Mac, in the app. No request a device can send starts, confirms or widens a pairing ([4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)).

### 3.4 What each side proves at every connection

**To the relay** (the relay path only): the Mac signs the relay's challenge with its relay key; the phone signs it with its device key and names its slot. The relay connects a phone only to its own pairing's Mac, and only while its slot is listed. While a pairing's Mac socket is connected, a second `hello` as that Mac is refused and, once it has signed the challenge with the relay key, the connected one is told, which the Devices page shows (*Another copy of pi² Remote tried to connect as this Mac*); the relay never replaces a Mac socket silently.

**To each other**, on either path, **Noise_KK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256**:[^noise] both static keys are known from pairing, so the handshake proves to each that the other holds its channel key, with forward secrecy once both handshake messages are done, in one round trip. The prologue is `pi2-v2`, the pairing's id and the device's id, so a handshake cannot be moved to another pairing or device. pi² Remote answers at most a few handshakes a minute for each device id, so a replayed first message costs it little.

**The Mac to the phone**: the Mac's first transport message carries its identity key's signature over the handshake hash and a fresh 32-byte challenge, which the phone checks against `i`. A copy of the Mac's Keychain keys without its Secure Enclave cannot pass ([3.2](#32-the-keys)).

**The device to the engine**, every time, on a new channel, whether it attaches afresh or resumes a session ([5.8](#58-resuming-and-moving-between-paths)): pi² Remote asks the engine `remote.attachBegin { session? }` on its control connection ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)), which answers a 32-byte challenge; it passes it to the phone, which answers `{"attach":{"signed":…,"signature":…,"presence":…}}`, with `signed` the JSON text `{"v":2,"kind":"attach","pairing":…,"device":…,"session":<null or the session to resume>,"binding":<this channel's handshake hash>,"challenge":…,"build":…,"at":…}` signed by the device key, and `presence` the presence key's signature over the same text, made under the session's Face ID ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)); pi² Remote sends it with the handshake hash it saw as `remote.attach`. **The engine** checks the device is paired, not revoked or suspended, and of a build at or above the minimum ([5.3](#53-the-relay)); both signatures, against the keys in its own record; the challenge it issued; the binding; and that `at` is within two minutes; and only then opens or resumes the session ([4.1](#41-from-the-app-or-from-a-paired-device)). pi² Remote also checks the signature against its own list of device keys and passes no line for a device not on it, so a device written into the engine's database by hand is not attached either. Neither check can be passed by pi² Remote alone, nor by the phone's channel key, which is not in the Secure Enclave, without the device key and the presence key, which are, and the second of which works only under a live Face ID.

**pi² Remote to the engine**: by being the engine's own child, not by its code signature. The engine starts pi² Remote with its control connection as an inherited socket (one end of a socket pair, as `pi2-peer` is given a connection as its file descriptor 3) and takes `remote.*` requests **only on the socket it made for the helper it started**, never on its listening socket, whatever PeerKit says of the process at the other end ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)).

A channel that has not attached within ten seconds of its handshake is closed.

### 3.5 How many devices

At most five devices are paired at once (question 8 in [section 14](#14-questions-for-the-owner)). Each has its own keys, slot at the relay, mailbox, push token and log; nothing one device does is visible to another but through the engine's state.

### 3.6 Revoking, and a lost phone

**Revoke** on the Devices page (`devices.revoke`, the app's alone):

1. sets `revokedAt` and forgets the device's keys in the same commit: from then on no attach, presence signature or sealed item of it is taken;
2. closes its device connections and channels at once;
3. has pi² Remote remove its slot at the relay, which closes its socket there, refuses it from then on, deletes its push token and its mailbox;
4. keeps its log for 90 days.

**Unpair this iPhone**, on the phone itself (its Settings), does the same from the other end, so that a person can take back everything about them without the Mac: the phone sends `devices.forgetSelf` if it can reach the Mac (a device's own request, [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)), which revokes it as above; it sends the relay `leave`, signed with its device key, which removes its slot, push token and mailbox whether the Mac is reachable or not; and it deletes its keys, cache, store and Outbox, every notification it has delivered, every Live Activity (ended), the widgets' file and the shared store, and unregisters from remote notifications. The Mac learns of a `leave` at its next connection to the relay and revokes the device then. This is the phone's "delete my data" ([9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store)).

Turning **Reach from an iPhone** off closes the channels and pi² Remote's socket at the relay; devices stay paired and can do nothing until it is on again. **Forget this Mac at the relay** (on the Devices page, with every device revoked) deletes the pairing there.

**A revocation the phone acts on** comes from the Mac, signed by its identity key: a `revoked` line on a channel, or a sealed notice in the mailbox. The phone acts on no other, so a relay that turns hostile cannot wipe phones by saying so. A revocation never deletes the Outbox without first showing what in it was not sent.

**The Mac suspends a device by itself** after five refused presence signatures or attaches in ten minutes: it takes nothing from it until the person resumes it on the Devices page (`devices.resume`, the app's alone), which says why.

**What a revoked or lost phone can still read from its own storage**:

- **Its cache**: the last state the phone saw (projects, the Dashboard, Needs you, the board's lists) and the last 300 entries of at most 20 conversations opened in the last seven days, with no attachments but those shown in them. It is kept with `NSFileProtectionComplete`, readable only while the phone is unlocked, and encrypted with the cache key, which opens only in a session Face ID opened ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)), so that a thief with the passcode and the phone in flight mode reads nothing. It is wiped when the phone learns it was revoked (from the Mac, as above), after **7 days** without hearing from the Mac, and when the app is deleted.
- **The widgets' file**: counts and names only, readable while locked, so a widget can be drawn.
- **Notifications already delivered**, each holding at most what [section 7](#7-notifications) allows.
- **Its own Outbox**.
- **Nothing in a backup**: the cache, the store, the Outbox and the widgets' file are excluded from iCloud and computer backups (`isExcludedFromBackup`); an iCloud backup without Advanced Data Protection is readable by Apple, and transcripts hold what agents read and ran.
- **Nothing in the app switcher**: the app covers its window when it goes to the background, so iOS's picture of it shows nothing.
- **Nothing at the relay**: its slot, mailbox and push token are deleted at revocation, and items sealed for another device cannot be opened with its keys.

A revoked phone that stays offline keeps its cache until the seven days pass; revoking cannot reach it. The notification service extension, which runs while the phone may be locked, never writes the store: it writes a small file of its own with `completeUnlessOpen`, which the app merges into the store when the phone is unlocked.

A lost phone that is not yet revoked can do what the table of [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send) allows to whoever holds it unlocked, on any network, within the relay's limits for its pairing ([6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost)), but only in a session that Face ID opened ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)): unlocked without the owner's face, it shows notifications and nothing more. **Revoking needs the Mac.** Away from the Mac, the remedy is Find My's **Erase iPhone**, which destroys the Secure Enclave's keys, and so every key that pairs the phone; the Devices page and the phone's first run both say so, and the Devices page shows when and by which path each device was last seen.

## 4. Who is speaking

### 4.1 From the app, or from a paired device

Today the engine takes an answer, or a change to what the person set, only from a connection whose other end is the app, as macOS's code signing says (`answers.ts`, [architecture.md](architecture.md#who-may-answer-for-the-person), #504, #513). This epic widens the rule to: **from the app, or from a paired device, as the device's table allows**.

- **Device sessions.** The engine takes devices' lines only from the pi² Remote it started itself, over the socket pair it made for it ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)). Each attached device's lines travel on that one control connection, tagged with a session id the engine gave at `remote.attach`, and the engine treats each session as a connection of its own (a `Client` with `device: string` set), with its own subscriptions and greeting. Nothing on the engine's listening socket can open, resume or speak in a session: a `remote.*` request there is refused whoever sends it, PeerKit's verdict included. A device session is never the app's: `connection.mayAnswer` answers `false` in it, with why.
- **Every request in a device session is checked against the device's table, whatever its class**: `deviceMay(method, params, session)` in `answers.ts` answers `"yes"`, `"face"` or `"no"`. `"no"` is refused ("This can be done only on the Mac, in the pi² app."); `"yes"` and `"face"` are handled as from the app: a session exists only once Face ID has opened it ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), so every request in it is under that Face ID, and there is no second one for any request (the owner's choice, [4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)). `"face"` marks a request that widens reach: such a request lives an hour in the mailbox, not a day, and the Mac notifies the person of it ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)). A request outside any session, with no attach, is refused ("Open pi² with Face ID."). The refusal comes before the handler runs, as today's does.
- A sealed item from the mailbox (`remote.mailboxIn`, [5.3](#53-the-relay)) is checked the same way, with the path `mailbox`, and refused unless its row allows the mailbox.
- A fourth list, `FOR_THE_HELPER`, holds the `remote.*` requests, taken only on the helper's control connection.
- **Every `devices.*` request but `devices.self` and `devices.forgetSelf` is in `FOR_THE_PERSON_SETTINGS`**, the app's alone, and so are turning remote access, the relay path, the direct switch and the preview on (`devices.set`); turning any of them off is open, as turning Allow everything off is. A command that could call `devices.pairStart` would be given `s`. The `devices` event is sent only to connections the engine takes for the app.
- The check at start-up widens: every handler belongs to exactly one of the three request classes as today, and to exactly one of the device lists (`FROM_A_DEVICE`, `WITH_FACE_ID`, `MAC_ONLY`), or to `FOR_THE_HELPER`; the engine will not open its socket otherwise. A request added later is placed by the issue that adds it, which tests its refusal from a device as `engine/test/answers.ts` tests the app's.
- Requests that are partly a device's, like `agents.allowEverything` (off, yes; on, the Mac's alone, question 5), have a function in the list, as `FOR_THE_PERSON` has.
- **P5's test lists every entry of `FOR_THE_PERSON` and `FOR_THE_PERSON_SETTINGS` that the table lets a device send, each with its one-line reason and whether it widens.** A new entry fails the test until it is placed in it, so nothing reaches a phone by default.

### 4.3 Face ID: the presence signature

Face ID opens a session; the presence signature is how the engine knows it did.

- **Opening.** When pi² comes to the foreground with no open session ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice) says when one ends), the app makes a new `LAContext` and asks Face ID with the policy for biometrics alone, with no passcode fallback. It then uses the presence key **with that context** for every signature of the session (CryptoKit's Secure Enclave keys take an authentication context[^seauth]): the attach, every resume, every item for the mailbox. Within the session no further prompt appears, because the context Face ID evaluated is reused. **When the session ends, the app invalidates the context**,[^lainvalidate] so the key cannot be used again until Face ID is asked again. There is no "Face ID passed" flag to forge: the engine sees only signatures that the Secure Enclave made under a live Face ID.
- **On a channel.** Every attach and every resume carries the presence key's signature over the same text the device key signs ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)): its binding to this channel, a challenge the engine issued, and `at` within two minutes. The engine opens or resumes the session only with both signatures. So a new channel, after the phone moved between networks or slept, carries requests only under a Face ID still live on the phone; a phone whose session ended must ask Face ID again before any channel carries its requests.
- **In a sealed item.** An item for the Mac's mailbox carries, inside, the request signed by the device key and by the presence key, as `{ v, request: { method, params }, signed, signature, presence }`, with `nonce` the item's id. An item whose request is a `"face"` one (a widening answer, [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)) is taken only within **one hour** of `at`, and the relay deletes it after an hour; any other within 24 hours. An answer that starts work past a limit, signed at noon, cannot be held back and delivered at three in the morning.
- `signed` is always the exact JSON text that was signed, so neither side has to put JSON in a canonical form. The engine checks the signatures with the device's keys (`node:crypto`). It keeps the nonces it took for 48 hours and takes none twice, after a restart too (`pi2.deviceNonces`).
- Five refused presence signatures or attaches in ten minutes suspend the device ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)).
- **Checked on a phone before it is built on**: that a Secure Enclave key with `biometryCurrentSet` signs again and again under one evaluated `LAContext` without a new prompt, until the context is invalidated. P12 checks it on the owner's phone first; if it does not hold, every new channel within a session asks Face ID again, which is safer and costs a prompt after a change of network.

### 4.4 The devices' log

Every request a device sends that is not in the Reading row of [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send), taken or refused, is logged in `pi2.deviceLog`; so are every attach and resume (with its path), every refused attach or presence signature, every suspension, and every `files.preview` and `conversation.export`, which copy data out:

```ts
type DeviceLogEntry = {
	at: number;
	device: string;
	path: "relay" | "home" | "direct" | "mailbox";
	method: string; // or "attach", "resume", "suspend"
	summary: string; // "Merged #617 “Sidebar badges” (pi2)"; made by `summarize(method, params)` in devices.ts
	presence: boolean; // the session or item was opened under a valid presence signature
	outcome: "done" | "refused" | "failed";
	why?: string; // the refusal's or the error's message
};
```

Entries are kept 90 days, at most 10,000 (the oldest go first). The Devices page shows them by device (`devices.log { device, before? }`, the app's), and the phone its own (`devices.self`). A method with no line in `summarize` is logged with its name and the project or conversation its parameters name; the issue that lets a device send a new request adds its line.

**What the person at the Mac sees**, so that someone driving the agents from across town is not invisible at the desk: while a device is attached, the menu bar item and the sidebar say so (*Alex's iPhone · through the relay*); and the Mac posts a notification when a pairing is claimed, when a device is paired, when a new Face ID key waits to be accepted, when a device sends a request that widens reach (the rows of [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send) that say so), and when a device is suspended. The Devices page also says when pi² Remote stopped unexpectedly more than three times in an hour.

### 4.5 A stolen or unlocked phone: the owner's choice

Whoever holds the phone unlocked, a thief who watched the passcode before taking it among them (the theft Apple's Stolen Device Protection is built against[^sdp]), could use the reach the agents already have: a message to an agent with Full access runs what it says, **Allow once** runs a waiting command, and every conversation can be read. Revoking needs the Mac ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)). The security reading (#635, M3) asked for this to be decided, and the owner answered on 21 November 2026:

> **"Face ID to open the app, nothing after."**

So:

- **Face ID opens pi² on the phone**: biometrics only, with no passcode fallback, since the case to guard against is someone who saw the passcode. What the phone keeps (its cache) opens only in an open session ([3.2](#32-the-keys)). Unlocked without the owner's face, the phone shows pi²'s notifications and nothing else: no conversation, no card, no answer.
- **Nothing is asked again inside an open session.** Every request the table of [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send) lets a phone send is sent with no second Face ID, the risky ones included.
- **A session ends** when pi² has been out of the foreground for **60 seconds**, when the phone locks, and when the app is ended or restarted; opening pi² again asks Face ID again. A banking app is the model: a minute to look something up in another app and come back, not long enough to leave the phone open on a table. The app invalidates the session's authentication context at that moment ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)) and tells the engine (`remote.sessionEnd`), which closes the device's session at once rather than keeping it 60 seconds for a resume.
- **A notification's action counts as opening.** Every action is a `foreground` action: it opens pi² on the card, asks Face ID if no session is open, shows the whole card, and then does what was chosen ([section 7](#7-notifications)). So no answer is ever given from the Lock Screen without Face ID, and none acts on text cut off in a notification.
- **What it costs.** A Face ID each time pi² is opened after a minute away. A person whose Face ID fails (a mask, an injury, Face ID locked out after failed tries until the passcode is entered) cannot use pi² on the phone until it works again; the Mac is unaffected. A phone without Face ID or Touch ID cannot be paired.
- **The remedy away from the Mac** is Find My's **Erase iPhone**, which destroys the Secure Enclave's keys, and so every key that pairs the phone; on the Mac, **Revoke**. The Devices page and the phone's first run both say so.
- **Whatever the session**: every App Intent sets `authenticationPolicy` to `.requiresAuthentication`, so Siri and Shortcuts do nothing on a locked phone,[^intentauth] and an intent that sends a request asks Face ID as opening the app does; widgets on the Lock Screen and in StandBy mark their content `privacySensitive`; the Mac suspends a device after five refused presence signatures or attaches in ten minutes.

**What was not taken, and what that leaves open.** The reading recommended Face ID to open each session *and* again for the risky answers inside it (the rows of 4.2 that widen reach). The owner chose the first alone. So within an open session everything a phone may send is one tap. A phone taken from the person's hand while pi² is open, or within a minute of leaving it, can until the session ends approve any waiting command, merge, change agents' settings, start past a limit, and message agents with Full access, with no further check. The Mac notifies the person of every widening request a device sends ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)), and keeps `agents.allowEverything` on to itself, pending question 5; the rest is the cost of the choice, said plainly here and in [section 11](#11-what-the-mac-cannot-check).

## 5. Transport

### 5.1 The shape: one channel, two paths

The phone and the Mac talk through **one secure channel**, a Noise session between the phone and pi² Remote ([5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise)), which carries the engine's own requests and events ([5.6](#56-the-messages)). The channel runs over either of two paths, and nothing above it knows which:

- **The relay**, the main path: both ends connect *out* to the relay over a WebSocket with TLS, which passes the channel's bytes between them. Any two networks that allow a web page to load allow it: a phone on a mobile network and a Mac at home, behind any NATs and firewalls, with nothing set up by the person.
- **Direct**, an optimisation: when the two can see each other (the same network, found by Bonjour; or an address of the Mac's that the phone can reach, such as a private network's), the same channel runs over TCP between them, which is faster and costs the relay nothing.

The same keys, the same handshake, the same messages and the same checks hold on both. The phone tries both when it connects and keeps whichever comes first, and moves a session from one to the other as the networks change ([5.8](#58-resuming-and-moving-between-paths)). The relay is the only path that must always work; direct is tried because it is cheaper, never because something needs it.

### 5.2 The secure channel: Noise

**The Noise Protocol Framework**,[^noise] with the handshakes `Noise_IKpsk2_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256` to pair ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)) and `Noise_KK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256` for every connection after ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)). Noise is what WireGuard is built on.[^wireguard] It is chosen over TLS because the channel must run inside the relay's WebSocket as well as over TCP, with either end in either role, and Apple's TLS (Network framework) runs only directly over TCP or UDP, with the connecting side always the client; and because both ends already know each other's keys from pairing, which Noise is built for and TLS would need certificates for.

- **Implemented once**, in `RemoteKit` ([section 8](#8-the-shared-package)), from the specification, on CryptoKit's Curve25519, ChaChaPoly and SHA-256, used by both pi² Remote and the phone. It is checked against the published Noise test vectors for these two protocols, and read in the step 0 and step 6 security readings (P1, P34). Nothing else of the cryptography is pi²'s own: Noise, HPKE (RFC 9180) and ECDSA P-256 are specified elsewhere, and CryptoKit implements their primitives.
- **Framing.** Each Noise message is at most 65,535 bytes.[^noise] On TCP, each is preceded by its length in two bytes; on the relay, each is one binary WebSocket message. Inside the channel, a frame is one byte of kind (`data`, `more`, `ack`, `ping`, `bye`) and its body; an engine line longer than a frame is split into `more` frames ending in a `data` frame.
- **Channel binding**: the handshake hash, which the device key signs at `pair`, `attach` and every resume, and the Mac's identity key at every connection ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)).
- **No handshake message carries a payload.** The first message of KK and IK can be replayed and is only weakly forward-secret,[^noisepayload] so the first data is always a transport message, after both handshake messages.
- **Padding**: a `data` frame may carry padding after its body, and the phone pads its frames to the next 256 bytes, so sizes say less.
- A channel lasts at most 24 hours, then is made again; it is never rekeyed. `bye` ends it, as Noise asks the application to signal the end of a session.
- **Parsers are fuzzed**: every part of RemoteKit and pi² Remote that reads bytes from the network (framing, handshake, frames, items, the pairing payload) is run against a fuzzer in CI before any external tester (P6, P34).
- **Byte by byte**: the prologues, the frames' bytes, the lines' exact forms, how signatures are written, the digits, the join token, the items' `info` and their contents are in [remotekit.md](remotekit.md), which P6 wrote with RemoteKit.

### 5.3 The relay

**What it is.** A Cloudflare Worker with one Durable Object for each pairing (P8), in this repository, in `relay/`. Each end holds one WebSocket to it; the pairing's object passes binary messages between the Mac's socket and each device's, keeps the pairing's small state, and sends pushes. It uses Durable Objects' WebSocket Hibernation, so a pairing whose sockets are idle costs nothing while they stay connected.[^dows]

**The relay's protocol**, inside `wss://<relay>/v1` (the address carries no id, since a platform's request logs keep URLs;[^cflogs] a connection names its pairing in a `Pi2-Pairing` header, which only hands it to the pairing's object, and its `hello` names it again; the messages' exact form is [relay/README.md](../relay/README.md#the-protocol)):

- **Hello.** The first text message names the role, the pairing and the end's build: `{ hello: { v: 1, role: "mac" | "device", pairing, device?, build, ext?: {} } }`, or `register` for a Mac with no pairing yet. Below the minimum build set for each role in the relay's configuration, the relay answers *update required* and closes ([6.5](#65-when-the-relay-is-down-or-the-person-wants-none) says how this turns it off for everyone). While a pairing's Mac socket is connected, a second Mac `hello` is refused, and the connected Mac is told if the second signed the challenge with the relay key, so that a stranger who knows the pairing's id cannot raise the alarm ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)). `ext` is empty today and ignored where it holds what the relay does not know; it is where a proof of purchase would go if the relay were ever paid for ([9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store)), so that needs no new protocol. The relay answers a 32-byte challenge, which the end signs with its relay key (the Mac) or device key (a phone); a phone's slot must be listed. A Mac's `register` gives its relay public key and signs the challenge; the relay answers the new pairing's id.
- **Data.** Binary messages: from a device, the channel's bytes; to the Mac, the same with the device's 16-byte id in front; from the Mac, with the id of the device it is for in front. The relay reads nothing past that id.
- **Control** (text messages): the Mac's alone are `openJoin { tokenHash, expires }`, `closeJoin`, `addDevice { device, deviceKey, attestKeyId }` (the phone's slot found by its device key, which the relay saw at its join, and `attestKeyId` the App Attest key the Mac checked, recorded but not tied), `removeDevice { device }`, `deletePairing`, `push { device, kind, … }` ([section 7](#7-notifications)) and `state { device, id, item }` (a device's latest state, with the item's id its reader needs to open it); both ends send `put { to, id, item, presence? }` (an item into the other's mailbox; the flag `presence`, named so on the wire, marks an item whose request is a `"face"` one ([4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)), which the relay keeps an hour, not a day: every item for the Mac carries a presence signature ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), and items the Mac writes for a device carry none) and `take` (an end asks for its mailbox); a device alone sends `token { apns, environment }` (its push token) and `leave` (removing itself, [3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)), signed with its device key.

**What it keeps**, per pairing, in its object's storage:

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| The pairing's id, the Mac's relay public key | To know the Mac | Until deleted, or 60 days with no connection |
| Each device's id, device public key, App Attest key id | To know a device | Until removed |
| Each device's push token and its environment | To send pushes | Until removed or replaced |
| The join window's token hash and expiry | Pairing | Five minutes |
| **The mailbox**: for the Mac, up to 200 sealed items, 1 MB in all, each at most 16 KB, and at most 50 items and 256 KB from any one device; for each device, the latest sealed state and up to 100 sealed items | So that what one end writes while the other is away is not lost: the phone's answers while the Mac sleeps; needs, receipts and the last state while the phone is unreachable | 24 hours each, one hour for an item marked `presence` (a `"face"` request), then deleted; an item is deleted once taken |
| Counters for the limits ([6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost)) | Limits | One day |

**Sealed items.** A mailbox item is sealed for its one reader with HPKE (RFC 9180) in its authenticated mode, DHKEM(P-256, HKDF-SHA256), HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM, as CryptoKit provides it:[^hpke] the Mac seals to the device's record key and authenticates with its own; the phone the other way. HPKE's `info` is `pi2-item-v2`, the pairing, the reader and the item's id, so an item cannot be moved and still open. **Every item is also signed by its writer**, inside the seal: by the Mac's identity key, or by the phone's device key. HPKE's sender authentication does not hold against someone who has the *reader's* private key,[^hpkekci] and the phone's record key is in the Keychain; the signature means a copy of it lets someone read the phone's items but not forge any from the Mac. The Mac's states carry a number that only rises, and the phone refuses a state older than one it has shown, so a relay cannot hand back an old one. An item from the phone carries, inside, a request signed by the device key and by the presence key, as `{ v, request: { method, params }, signed, signature, presence }` with `signed` = `{ v, kind: "request", pairing, device, method, params, nonce: <item id>, at }`. The Mac takes such an item only if it carries both signatures, if `at` is at most 24 hours old (one hour for a `"face"` request, [4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), its nonce is new, its row of [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send) allows the mailbox, and (for an answer) the item still waits; it writes back a sealed receipt either way ("This is no longer waiting: it was answered or withdrawn." or "Not sent: more than a day passed before your Mac saw it.").

**Many pairings, nobody's in particular.** The relay is written for any number of pairings from the start, each in its own object with its own limits ([6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost)), even while it serves the owner's one. A pairing is identified by its keys and a random id, never by an account, a name, an e-mail address or an Apple Account: the relay has no accounts. Nothing of the owner's is in its code; what a deployment needs (its push key, the team and app ids App Attest checks against, its limits, its address) is its configuration and its secrets ([6.4](#64-what-the-owner-sets-up-once)), so another deployment, the person's own or a future paid one, is the same code with other settings.

**What the relay knows**: a pairing's id, the public keys it checks, its devices' ids, count and push tokens, when each end connects and from which address (the Mac's home address and the phone's network's, while connected; it stores none), and the size and time of every message, item and push, which show when the person works, how long answers stream and how many cards they get. Durable Objects on SQLite keep 30 days of point-in-time recovery,[^dopitr] so what the relay deletes can be brought back by whoever controls its account for 30 days; the privacy policy says so. (A slot brought back that way gives nothing: the Mac forgot the device's keys.) **What it cannot know**: anything inside a channel or a sealed item. The one exception, which the person turns on for each Live Activity, is a Live Activity's counts ([7](#7-notifications)). Cloudflare, which runs it, sees the same, and terminates the outer TLS; it cannot read inside Noise or HPKE.

**What it must never log**: a message's or an item's bytes, a push's payload, a push token, a pairing's or a device's id with an address, or any key. Workers Logs and invocation logs are off in its configuration,[^cflogs] it writes nothing to the console, and a check in CI fails if either changes (P15). The only numbers it keeps are the counters of its limits and the per-hour totals its operator's bill needs. **Only authenticated traffic counts against a pairing's limits**: a stranger who learned a pairing's id cannot spend its limits and get its sockets closed.

### 5.4 Direct, at home

- Every path works on IPv6 alone, as App Review requires:[^ipv6] the relay is reached by name, over Cloudflare's network, which answers on IPv6; pi² Remote listens on both IPv4 and IPv6, and the Mac's addresses it sends include its IPv6 ones.
- **Who the listener takes.** Many home networks give a Mac a public IPv6 address, and a laptop carries the listener into every café and hotel. So pi² Remote takes connections only from link-local, private (RFC 1918) and unique-local addresses, and from the shared range private networks use (100.64.0.0/10, as Tailscale does), unless the person turns **Direct from any address** on, on the Devices page (off by default). It holds at most eight connections before they authenticate, ten seconds each, and answers at most a few handshakes a minute for each device id.
- pi² Remote listens on TCP while remote access is on and **Find on this network** is on, in a release build, and advertises `_pi2._tcp` with a TXT record `v=2` and `t=<tag>`, where the tag is HMAC-SHA256, keyed with a key of the pairing, of the day's date, cut to 8 bytes ([remotekit.md](remotekit.md#the-home-tag) gives its bytes): a paired phone recognises its Mac; nobody else learns the pairing's id, which would follow the Mac from network to network and name it at the relay. The instance name is the Mac's name, as macOS gives it to Bonjour already. A development build or throwaway copy listens only on the loopback interface and advertises nothing ([10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)).
- The phone lists `_pi2._tcp` in `NSBonjourServices` and has an `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`; browsing asks the phone's owner once for Local Network.[^lnp] It asks while in the foreground, during pairing, since an app in the background with the privilege undetermined is denied without being asked.[^lnp]
- The channel on this path is the same Noise channel as on the relay, on TCP, with the length framing of [5.2](#52-the-secure-channel-noise). A listener reached from anywhere answers only a Noise handshake with a paired device's static key, and closes anything else after ten seconds.
- The Mac sends its current addresses inside the channel whenever they change (the `device` event, [5.6](#56-the-messages)), so the phone can try them from any network: `home` when found by Bonjour, `direct` otherwise. A private network of the person's (Tailscale and the like) is used this way if they have one, with nothing set up in pi²; nothing relies on it.

### 5.5 Which program holds them

The Mac's ends of both paths, its keys, and the channels are held by **pi² Remote**, a small app bundle inside the app's (`Contents/Helpers/pi² Remote.app`, bundle id `dev.pi2.app.remote`, no Dock icon), written in Swift (P7, P10). **The engine starts it**, whenever remote access is on and a device is paired or a pairing is open; it starts it again if it dies (waiting twice as long after each failure, up to a minute), and stops it when remote access goes off or the engine stops. So the Mac is reachable exactly while the engine runs, which outlives the app's window (the login item starts the engine with no app at all).

Why a helper, and not the engine itself:

- **The cryptography is Apple's.** Noise on CryptoKit's primitives, HPKE, the Secure Enclave and the data-protection Keychain are Swift-only; the engine (Node) would have to implement HPKE itself, and could keep its keys only where any command of the person's can read them (`/usr/bin/security`, [architecture.md](architecture.md#who-may-answer-for-the-person)).
- **macOS's Local Network privilege follows code signing.** Bonjour on macOS needs it, and a launchd agent (the engine's login item) is not exempt; macOS asks once, naming the responsible app.[^lnp] A signed bundle of pi²'s with its own usage description is asked once, by name, and the answer stays. The relay path asks for nothing: it is an outgoing connection to the internet.
- **One implementation of the channel**, shared with the phone through `RemoteKit`.

And not the app: the app may be quit while the engine works.

**The engine's own child, steered by nothing else.** Every agent's command runs as the person and can start a copy of `pi² Remote.app` itself, which passes any check of its code signature. So:

- **The engine starts it with its control connection as an inherited socket**, one end of a socket pair it made, as file descriptor 3 (as `pi2-peer` is given a connection), and knows its process id. It starts one at a time. It takes `remote.*` requests, and device sessions ([4.1](#41-from-the-app-or-from-a-paired-device)), **only on that socket**, never on its listening socket, whatever PeerKit says of the process at the other end. Every device's lines travel on that one connection, tagged by session.
- **A pi² Remote not started by the engine exits before it reads any key.** At start it checks that file descriptor 3 is a Unix socket whose other end is its parent process, that the parent's process id is the one in the data folder's `engine.pid`, and that the parent's code satisfies the requirement of the Node the app's bundle carries; otherwise it exits. The relay, the socket, the mode and the keys all come from the engine over the control connection: **a release build of pi² Remote reads no arguments, environment variables or files that choose any of them**. Loopback mode, the key file and a relay on this Mac ([10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)) exist only in builds that are not signed with pi²'s Developer ID, and the helper checks its own signature to know which it is.
- **The relay never replaces the Mac's socket silently** ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)): a copy that read the keys could not quietly take the pairing at the relay while the real helper is connected, and the Devices page would say so if it tried.
- **pi² Remote keeps its own list of the paired devices' keys** in its Keychain, written only when the engine pairs a device over the control connection, and attaches no device outside it ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)).
- **P5's tests**: a `remote.attach`, `remote.pairClaim` or any other `remote.*` request sent on the engine's ordinary socket, by a process that passes PeerKit as pi² Remote, is refused. **P7's tests**: pi² Remote started with no inherited socket, or with one whose other end is not the engine of `engine.pid`, exits before it opens the Keychain.

**What an agent on the Mac can and cannot reach**, with this in place:

- It **cannot** speak in a device session, open one, or resume one: those exist only on the engine's socket pair with its own helper. It cannot read the Mac's keys through `/usr/bin/security`, nor through a copy of pi² Remote, which exits first. It cannot read `s` or a pairing's claims: they go to the app's connection only ([3.1](#31-what-the-mac-shows-and-the-phone-scans)). It cannot pair a device of its own: pairing takes the digits the person's phone shows, typed by the person, and an App Attest attestation the Mac checks ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)).
- It **can** kill pi² Remote, which cuts the phone off until the engine starts it again, and which the Devices page reports when it happens often. It can read what it could read before: conversations, through the engine's open requests.
- **Going round pi² on purpose** stays out of reach of these checks, as [projects.md 7.3](projects.md#73-who-answered) says: a command that rewrites the data folder (`engine.pid`, the database) or stops the engine and starts one of its own can defeat them. pi² Remote's own list of device keys keeps even that from attaching a device the person did not pair.

**The engine stays the judge, and pi² Remote holds the device's authority while it is attached.** Every rule of [section 4](#4-who-is-speaking) is checked in the engine, and a fault in pi² Remote cannot make the engine take a request the device's table refuses, nor a Face ID request without the device's own signature. But once a device is attached, the engine takes from pi² Remote any request the table allows, as the device; that is why it must be the engine's own child, steered by nothing else. pi² Remote talks to the engine on its control connection (`remote.*` requests, device sessions, and the `remote` event the engine sends only on it, which carries pushes to send and items to seal).

**When it asks macOS for anything.** Only the `home` path's Bonjour advertising, in a release build, once, when the person first turns remote access on; the Devices page says so first. If the macOS firewall is on, it may also ask whether pi² Remote may accept incoming connections: P7 finds out, on the owner's Mac and with the owner's word, what a Developer ID-signed helper is asked, and writes it here. With **Find on this network** off, nothing is asked at all and the relay carries everything.

### 5.6 The messages

**The engine's own requests and events, carried as they are**: one JSON line each, inside the channel, in both directions, as on the socket ([architecture.md](architecture.md#how-they-talk)). The shared package's client already speaks them ([section 8](#8-the-shared-package)), so the phone gets every request and event as the Mac app has them, and nothing has to be kept in step with a second protocol. What a phone may send is limited in the engine ([4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)); what it is sent, in the engine too ([5.7](#57-what-is-never-sent-to-a-phone)).

On top of them, only:

- the first lines of a channel: `pair`, or `challenge` and `attach`, answered by `paired` or `attached`, and `resume` ([5.8](#58-resuming-and-moving-between-paths));
- `presence` beside the attach and every resume ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), and `sessionEnd` when the phone's session ends ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice));
- a `revoked` line, signed by the Mac's identity key, before a revoked device's channel is closed ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone));
- a `refused` line in place of `attached`, before the channel is closed, naming why from a fixed list (an old build, a suspended device, a refused presence signature, the clocks apart, a session that cannot be resumed), never in the engine's words: so the phone stops retrying where retrying cannot help ([remotekit.md](remotekit.md#the-refused-line));
- a `device` event, sent to a device session after the greeting and whenever its record changes: `{ event: "device", id, name, pairing, mac: { name }, addresses: string[], relay: boolean, revoked: boolean }`. A revoked device is sent `revoked: true` and closed.

The greeting in a device session is the app's ([projects.md](projects.md#44-what-an-app-is-sent-on-connecting)), less [5.7](#57-what-is-never-sent-to-a-phone)'s events, then `device`.

### 5.7 What is never sent to a phone

- **Events**: `computer`, `browser`, `auth`, `local`, `local.progress`, `mcp` and `devices` are not sent in a device session (the app's host roles, sign-ins, the Mac's upkeep, and pairings and devices' keys, [3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)).
- **Where a secret often hides**: MCP servers' command lines and endpoints' addresses, which often carry a token typed outside the secret fields, are sent to a device by name only (`providers` and any event that names them).
- **Secrets**: no event or answer carries a provider's key, a sign-in's token, an agent's variable's value or an MCP server's variables today, and none may. P5 adds a test that runs a demo session with known secrets set and fails if any of their values appears in a line sent to a device connection.
- **The test copy's key**, and anything from `test-copy.*`.
- **Files outside what a conversation shows**, but through `files.preview`: a text file that the conversation's transcript links to, under its own folder, at most 256 KB, refused for anything that is not UTF-8 text, that git ignores (an `.env`, a `credentials.json`), or that lies outside the folder after links are followed (P29).
- **Attachments the phone sends** are written on the Mac under names the engine makes, never the names the phone gave.

What *is* sent: conversations as their transcripts hold them, which include what agents read and ran. That is the product. It travels only inside the channel.

### 5.8 Resuming, and moving between paths

- **A session** is the phone's attachment to the engine. pi² Remote keeps a device's engine connection for 60 seconds after its channel drops, and keeps the engine lines it sent that the phone has not acknowledged (the phone sends `ack` with the count it has, every second or 64 KB), up to 4 MB.
- **Resuming**: on a new channel, by either path, the phone attaches with the session it wants to resume and the count of lines it has received ([3.4](#34-what-each-side-proves-at-every-connection)): a **new** signature by the device key and by the presence key, under the Face ID that opened the session ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), over this channel's handshake hash and a challenge **the engine** issued for it, with the session's id. The engine checks it as any attach (the device not revoked or suspended, the binding, the challenge), logs the resume ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)), and moves the session to the new channel; pi² Remote then sends what the phone has not had and carries on, without a new greeting. Nothing of a resume is decided by pi² Remote alone, and the phone's channel key, which is not in the Secure Enclave, cannot resume a session without its device key and its presence key, which are; and the presence key signs only while the session's Face ID is live. A session that ended on the phone ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)) cannot be resumed at all. If the session is gone or its buffer was passed, it answers `resume: false` and the phone attaches afresh: the engine sends the greeting, as to an app that reconnects after the engine restarted, the phone asks again for the conversations it has open (`conversation.open` with `newestFirst`), and a message whose answer never came is sent again with the same request id, which the engine already takes without doubling it ([architecture.md](architecture.md#keeping-the-app-quick-while-agents-work)).
- **Moving**: on a relay channel, the phone tries the addresses of the `device` event in the background; once a direct channel resumes the session (a new attach, as above), the session moves to it and the relay channel is closed. When a direct channel drops (the phone left home), the phone resumes over the relay at once. A streamed answer goes on across the move with at most the lines in flight sent twice, which the phone drops by their count.
- **After the phone slept**: it holds no channel in the background beyond the short time iOS gives it,[^bgtime] and on coming back resumes if it can, or attaches afresh. The Outbox is sent before anything new.

### 5.9 Battery and data

- The phone holds a channel only while in the foreground, and for the short times iOS wakes it (a notification's action, a background push). It never polls.
- The Mac holds one WebSocket to the relay at all times while remote access is on, with a ping every 30 seconds to keep its network's NAT open; the relay's object hibernates between messages.[^dows]
- The engine sends `activity` to a device connection at most once a second (to the app, four times), and `conversation` events only for conversations the phone has open, as for the app.
- A greeting is the size of the person's library and projects; P35 measures it, and a request for only what changed since a version is left to an issue of its own if it matters.
- P35 measures a day's battery and data for an ordinary day (the app open two hours, sixty notifications); this page promises no number.

## 6. Across any two networks: the ways compared

### 6.1 The three ways

| | (a) A relay pi² runs | (b) Peer to peer (STUN, TURN when needed) | (c) The person's iCloud only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Any two networks** | Yes: both ends connect out over HTTPS, which every network allows | Mostly: a direct path through both NATs when they allow one, a TURN relay when not | Yes |
| **The live conversation** | Yes, a few tens of milliseconds behind the Mac | Yes; lower latency when direct | **No.** Each change is a record saved and fetched in a round trip; pushes about changes are merged and may drop data;[^dbsub] silent pushes are not guaranteed and are throttled past two or three an hour;[^bgpush] saving too often is refused with a time to wait.[^cklimit] A streamed answer would arrive in lumps, seconds apart at best, with no guarantee |
| **Pushes** | The relay sends them with pi²'s push key | Need a sender anyway: a relay, or iCloud | iCloud sends them for the Mac's records |
| **What else it needs** | Nothing more | Signalling, to exchange addresses before the first packet: the small relay of (a), or iCloud. Apple's frameworks have no ICE, so it needs Google's WebRTC library or an ICE of pi²'s own | Both devices on the same Apple Account; CloudKit and push entitlements on the Mac |
| **How often the fallback is needed** | — | Published measurements: about 70% of hole-punching attempts succeed;[^libp2p] 17.7% of one service's peer-to-peer calls needed TURN, with no figures published since 2017;[^turnpct] mobile networks put phones behind carrier-grade NAT, through which a direct path often cannot be made.[^tsnat] Plan for a third of phone sessions relayed, and for ICE to run again on every change of network | — |
| **What it costs to run** | Cloudflare: see [6.2](#62-what-the-relay-costs) | TURN at Cloudflare costs $0.05 a GB past 1,000 GB a month, shared;[^turn] a thousand people's relayed third is about 130 GB a month, so nothing; plus the signalling relay of (a) | Nothing to pi²: the private database is the person's own iCloud storage[^ck] |
| **What the owner sets up** | A Cloudflare account and a push key ([6.4](#64-what-the-owner-sets-up-once)) | The same, and TURN credentials | A CloudKit container |
| **Upkeep** | None to patch; deploys from CI | All of (a), plus a peer-to-peer stack on both ends to keep working as networks and NATs change | None |

**(a) on Fly.io instead**, a small container running the same relay: the smallest machine that stays on costs $2.19 a month, data out $0.02 a GB in North America and Europe, and there is no free tier;[^fly] for a thousand people about $16 a month (two machines in two regions, and about 400 GB of data out). It is a server to keep: its runtime to update, a restart that drops every socket, and, once there is more than one machine, a way to route a phone to the machine its Mac is on.

### 6.2 What the relay costs

On Cloudflare's Workers Paid plan, $5 a month with its included use, or the Free plan:[^cfworkers][^dopricing]

- A Durable Object is billed per request (1 million a month included, then $0.15 a million), where each WebSocket message it *receives* counts as one twentieth of a request and messages it sends count nothing; and per GB-second it is awake (400,000 included, then $12.50 a million), where an object waiting with hibernating sockets counts nothing. Data out costs nothing.[^dopricing][^cfworkers]
- The Free plan includes 100,000 requests a day and 13,000 GB-seconds a day, and fails operations past them.[^dopricing]

For one person on an ordinary day (the app open two hours, receiving about three engine lines a second while open; sixty pushes; thirty-five connections): about 23,000 messages received, so about 1,190 billed requests a day and 7 GB-seconds a day (2 ms awake for each message, 200 ms for each push's call to Apple). So:

| | Requests a month | GB-seconds a month | Cost a month |
|---|---|---|---|

### 6.3 The recommendation

**(a), a relay pi² runs, on Cloudflare Workers with Durable Objects**, which also sends the pushes.

- Only a relay carries a live conversation across any two networks with nothing for the person to set up, and (b) needs one anyway, for signalling and for the third of sessions it cannot connect.
- (b)'s gain, lower latency on a direct path, matters for voice and video, not for text events a few times a second; its cost is a peer-to-peer stack (Google's WebRTC library, or ICE of pi²'s own) on both ends, and a phone that must redo ICE whenever it changes network.
- (c) cannot stream ([6.1](#61-the-three-ways)).
- Cloudflare over Fly.io: no server to keep, sockets that cost nothing while idle, nothing to pay for one person and about $10 a month for a thousand, its network near both ends, and tests that run it locally (`wrangler`, the Workers Vitest integration[^cfvitest]). The relay's protocol is plain WebSockets, so it can move to a container later without changing either end.

**Pushes come from the relay**, not from iCloud, now that the relay is there anyway: a push is sent at once with Apple's high priority rather than merged with others;[^apns] it needs no shared Apple Account and no CloudKit on the Mac; and only a holder of the push key can update a Live Activity while the app is closed,[^lapush] which the relay can. Its content is sealed for the phone and opened by the phone's notification service extension ([section 7](#7-notifications)).

### 6.4 What the owner sets up once

1. **A Cloudflare account that holds the relay and nothing else**, on the Workers Paid plan ($5 a month) with a spending limit and an e-mail alert, or the Free plan while the owner is the only one using it.[^cfworkers][^cfspend]
2. **An API token** for deploying Workers, scoped to Workers on that one account, kept **only in GitHub environments** `relay-development` and `relay-production`, each limited to `main` with the owner as required reviewer ([12.3](#123-before-anything-is-built-the-repository-p0)); never a repository secret, which a workflow run from any branch could read.[^ghenv] Or the owner deploys by hand with `wrangler deploy`.
3. **An APNs authentication key** (a `.p8` file) from the Apple developer account, **topic-specific, for `dev.pi2.ios` only**, and for one environment: a sandbox key for the development relay, a production key for the production relay.[^apnskey] A team-scoped key could push to every app of the team. Its key id and the team id are given to the relay as Workers secrets (`APNS_KEY`, `APNS_KEY_ID`, `APNS_TEAM_ID`) by the owner with `wrangler secret put`: **the key is never in GitHub**, nor in the repository.[^cfsecrets] The team id and the app's id are also what the relay checks App Attest against; they are settings of the deployment, not written in its code. Each relay also has two secrets of its own from the start, `APP_ATTEST_TEAM_ID` and `ADDRESS_KEY`, added in Cloudflare's dashboard once it is deployed; every other setting is in `relay/wrangler.jsonc` (P9, [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#deploying-the-relay)).
4. **The app's records**: the App IDs of pi² for iPhone and its extensions with Push Notifications and App Attest, pi² Remote's with Keychain sharing, and the app in App Store Connect for TestFlight (P14).
5. **A domain for the production relay** (`relay.<domain>`) on the same account, which a rate limit on its connections needs, and the production relay alone has: a workers.dev address takes no rules of the owner's. The development relay uses the address Cloudflare gives (`pi2-relay-development.<subdomain>.workers.dev`) (P9).

pi² does the rest: the relay's code and its configuration in `relay/`; a workflow that tests it on every pull request and deploys it, to a development relay and to the production one, when the owner runs it and approves the deployment; the relay's address built into release builds (and a field on the Devices page for another); every key, made at pairing; registering each pairing; and nothing for the person to set up on the Mac or the phone beyond pairing. `docs/relay.md` (P9) is the owner's page for the list above.

### 6.5 When the relay is down, or the person wants none

- **At home it still works**, directly: the whole of pi² over the `home` path, with the same channel.
- **Away, over a private network** the person has (Tailscale and the like), it works directly too, at the Mac's addresses.
- **Otherwise, away**: the phone shows "pi²'s relay can't be reached", with its last state dated, and keeps what is written in the Outbox until a path returns.
- **No pushes** while the relay is down. When it returns, the Mac sends pushes only for what still waits, not the ones it missed.
- **The person who wants none** turns **Reach through pi²'s relay** off on the Devices page: home and private networks only, and no notifications while the app is closed.
- **The person's own relay**: the code in `relay/` deploys to anyone's Cloudflare account, and the Devices page takes its address. It passes channels and keeps mailboxes as pi²'s does. It can send pushes only to a build of the app signed by the same team as its push key, so a TestFlight or App Store build gets notifications only through pi²'s relay.
- **Turning it off for everyone**, when a fault is found in RemoteKit, pi² Remote or the relay: [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#turning-it-off-for-everyone) has a section for it (P9). Take a setting away from each relay (its secret `ADDRESS_KEY`, in Cloudflare's dashboard): a relay missing a setting refuses every new connection and closes each open one at its next message, and no deploy puts a secret back; then raise the minimum builds above every one in use ([5.3](#53-the-relay)) before turning it back on; revoke the APNs key in the developer account; expire the TestFlight build; ship a Mac update with remote access off. pi² Remote also refuses a phone below a minimum build on every path, the home path included, and the phone refuses a Mac below one, so an old version can be shut out without the relay. `SECURITY.md` names where to report a fault in the iPhone app, pi² Remote or the relay (P9).
- Not built in this epic: a fallback that carries notifications through the person's iCloud when the relay is down (the first draft of this page describes one); it can be filed once the relay has run for a while, if it is wanted.

### 6.6 Abuse and cost

**Who can use the relay.**

- A Mac registers a pairing by proving its relay key. A pairing stays **pending** and is deleted after 15 minutes unless a phone that joined it is added (the relay adds no other, [relay/README.md](../relay/README.md#what-it-decides)), and a phone joins only with an App Attest attestation of pi² for iPhone, signed by pi²'s team, checked by the relay against Apple's root,[^attest] together with the join token from the Mac's QR code. So the relay carries traffic only between a Mac and a genuine pi² for iPhone paired with it; anyone else can at most make pending pairings, which cost a row for 15 minutes and a few hundred writes at most: until its first phone is added, a pairing's Mac has a small allowance of its own (10 messages a second, 200 a day), and every message it sends counts.
- Registering is limited per address: 5 an hour, 20 a day; and so is connecting at all ([the table](#66-abuse-and-cost) below). An IPv6 address counts by its /64, which a home or a phone network is given whole. The address is held only as a keyed hash, with a key that changes daily, for the day.
- Under decision 3's recommended answer, only the owner's TestFlight builds exist, so only the owner's phone can join anything.
- App Attest proves a genuine copy of the app, not the person's phone. Once the app is public, anyone can pair a genuine copy with a "Mac" of their own making and use the relay as a free tunnel within one pairing's limits. The limits below are what bound that, so they matter more than "only genuine apps" suggests. Binding the attestation to the device key ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)) keeps a script from bringing a key of its own.
- Only authenticated traffic counts against a pairing's limits ([5.3](#53-the-relay)).

**Limits for each pairing**, counted for each of its ends apart (the Mac, each phone, and much less for a phone that has only joined a window), so that no end's traffic holds off another, and, once a phone has been added, the Mac's `removeDevice`, `closeJoin` and `deletePairing` are not counted when they take a device, an open window or the pairing away (and are counted when there is nothing to take; a pending pairing's Mac has the small allowance above, and all it sends counts): past a limit, the relay closes that end's socket with "busy" and when it may come back, rather than drop a message from the middle of its channel, and the end reconnects and resumes ([5.8](#58-resuming-and-moving-between-paths)); past its day's limit, an end is held off for an hour at a time (a phone's hello too; the Mac's hello is taken, but its counted messages are held off), and the Mac's Devices page says why:

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Sockets | 6 (the Mac and five devices) |
| Connections from one address (an IPv6 address by its /64), all pairings together | 120 a minute, 1,200 an hour; past them, HTTP 429 with `Retry-After`. A fence against one address opening thousands: a household reconnecting after its network dropped makes a few dozen ([relay/README.md](../relay/README.md#what-it-decides)) |
| Join windows opened | 10 a minute for each pairing; past them, "busy" |
| A message | 70 KB (a Noise message and its header) |
| Messages | 100 a second; 300,000 a day |
| Data | 2 MB a second; 500 MB a day |
| Mailbox | As [5.3](#53-the-relay) |
| Pushes | 120 alerts an hour per device, 60 background pushes, 60 Live Activity updates |

**Bounding the bill**: a spending limit on the Cloudflare account and an e-mail alert;[^cfspend] the relay answers "busy" rather than failing when a limit is reached.

**What a lost phone can do through the relay before it is revoked**: what [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send) allows, if unlocked (the device key works only then), on any network, within its pairing's limits, but only in a session its owner's face opened ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)). It can reach no other pairing, read no other device's mailbox, and send pushes to nobody (only the Mac can ask for a push). Revoking removes it from the relay at once ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)).

**A photographed QR code** is not enough to pair: it also takes a genuine pi² for iPhone, which the Mac checks itself, the only claim within five minutes, and the person typing on the Mac the digits their own phone shows ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)).

## 7. Notifications

**Who sends them**: the engine decides, pi² Remote seals, the relay sends. The engine sends pi² Remote a `remote` event `{ push: { device, kind, title, body, need?, projectId? } }`; pi² Remote seals it to the device's record key and asks the relay to `push`; the relay signs a token with its APNs key and posts it to Apple's push service.[^apns][^apnskey]

**What triggers one.**

- A new item in Needs you (with the projects preview on; the Inbox's items with it off), one notification per item, for each paired device.
- A provider's limit reached, or work carrying on past it, as the Mac notifies today (`Notifier.limitNotices`).
- An orchestrator's finished answer, only for a message the person sent from the phone with **Tell me when it answers** on.
- When an item is answered or withdrawn, a background push so the phone takes its notification away (best effort; Apple may hold or drop these[^bgpush]), and the item's removal in the device's mailbox.

The phone's own settings turn each of the first three off.

**What the push carries.** An alert push with a generic alert ("pi²", "Something needs you"), `mutable-content` set, a `collapse-id` that is an HMAC of the item's id with a key of the pairing (the item's id itself would be seen by the relay and Apple), and one custom key, `sealed`: the notification's content sealed with HPKE for the device and signed by the Mac's identity key ([5.3](#53-the-relay)), padded to 1, 2 or 4 KB so that its size does not tell an approval from a question, within Apple's 4 KB.[^payload] No category, no thread, no project and no kind are visible outside the seal: the notification service extension sets them when it opens it.[^nse] The relay sees that an alert of so many bytes went to a device at a time, and nothing else.

**What the text may contain** (inside the seal):

- Title: the agent's name and the kind ("Forge needs approval", "Scribe has a question", "The orchestrator asks for more reach", "Forge proposes a rule", "Forge is held back by a limit"), as the Mac's `Notifier.needsYou` says it.
- Body: the project's name and the card's short line (`NeedItem.short`, the tool and its call's summary for an approval), at most 180 characters, after the engine has replaced with "•••" every secret it knows that appears in it (agents' variables' values, providers' keys and tokens, MCP servers' variables) and every string shaped like a key or a token (a long run of high-entropy characters; known prefixes such as `ghp_`, `github_pat_`, `sk-`, `xox`, `AKIA`), since agents put tokens they read from files into commands.
- Never: a command's output, a file's contents, a diff, an attachment, or any transcript text beyond the card's own short line.
- **On the Lock Screen**, the phone's own setting **Details on the Lock Screen** is off by default: each category has a `hiddenPreviewsBodyPlaceholder` ("Forge needs approval" without the command), shown until the phone is unlocked. iOS's own previews setting applies on top.

**The notification service extension** (P21) opens `sealed` with the record key, which is usable after the first unlock, within the thirty seconds or so iOS allows it,[^nse] and checks the Mac's signature; then sets the title, the body, the category of the item's kind and its thread (the project). **It always replaces the title and the body**: if `sealed` is missing, does not open, or does not verify, it shows *pi²: something needs you*, whatever the push's own alert said, so neither a hostile relay nor a leaked push key can put words on a pi² notification. It writes the item to a small file of its own (`completeUnlessOpen`), which the app merges into its store when the phone is unlocked ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)). While the phone is offline, Apple keeps only the last notification for the app;[^apns] the phone takes its mailbox whenever it next connects, so nothing is lost but the banner.

**Its actions, by kind.** A notification's action counts as opening pi² ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)). So **every action is `foreground` and `authenticationRequired`**: iOS asks for the phone to be unlocked,[^auth] then opens pi² on the card;[^fg] pi² asks Face ID if no session is open, shows the whole card, and does what was chosen, with no further tap. The buttons save finding the card; they never answer from the Lock Screen, since Face ID cannot be asked for in the background.

| Kind | Category | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Approval | `pi2.approval` | **Allow once**, **Deny** (destructive), **Open** (Always Allow and rules are on the card in the app). **Allow once is offered only when the body shows the whole call, uncut**; a longer call gets **Open** and **Deny** only. And Allow once acts only after the app has shown the whole call on the card, so a harmful end cut off after a harmless start is never allowed unseen |
| Question | `pi2.question` | **Reply** (a text input action, offered only when the question is shown whole; sent once pi² is open under Face ID), **Open** |
| Decision | `pi2.decision` | **Open** (categories are fixed when the app starts, so a decision's own choices cannot be buttons) |
| More reach | `pi2.reach` | **Allow…**, **Refuse** |
| Proposed rule | `pi2.rule` | **Accept** (offered only when the rule's text is shown whole), **Refuse**, **Open** (to edit) |
| Limit | `pi2.limit` | **Start anyway…**, **Wait** |
| Notice | `pi2.notice` | none |

**What the phone does to deliver an answer.** iOS opens the app with the action;[^actions] once Face ID has opened the session and the card has been shown, the app:

1. makes the request and puts it in the Outbox;
2. if the person leaves pi² at once, asks iOS for more time to finish (`beginBackgroundTask`);[^bgtime]
3. connects (relay and home at once, four seconds), attaches, sends the request and waits for the answer;
4. if the Mac is not connected to the relay, puts it in the Mac's mailbox as a sealed item, signed by the device key and the presence key ([4.3](#43-face-id-the-presence-signature)), and stops there: the receipt comes later;
5. takes the notification away, and leaves the Outbox entry until the Mac's answer or receipt says what became of it. If even step 4 could not be done before iOS ended the app's time, the app sends it the next time a session opens (an item older than its limit is signed again then), and a local notification tells the person once: "Your answer to Forge is not sent yet. pi² will send it when it can reach your Mac."

**A Live Activity** ([design](design/iphone.md#what-a-phone-adds)) is started on the phone, which sets its static part (the project's name) there; the relay updates it while the app is closed with Live Activity pushes,[^lapush] whose content Apple's system reads directly, so it cannot be sealed. So a Live Activity's pushes carry **numbers only**: `{ working, needs, finished, at }`. The relay can read those numbers. The person turns on each Live Activity, and the switch says so.

**The conversation's page on the phone shows what agents wrote, so it can do nothing else** (P4, P18). It has a content security policy with no inline script and no remote content; its bridge to the app carries no message that becomes an engine request: answers, presence signatures and messages are made only by the app's own controls, outside the page. A link in a transcript is opened only after the person confirms it, and `pi2:` links in a transcript are not followed. Otherwise an agent that got script into its own text could answer its own card through the person's phone.

**Only public interfaces.** Both the shared targets and the phone use Apple's public APIs alone,[^publicapi] and no framework a phone app may not ship; the Mac app keeps its own exceptions (Accessibility for computer use), which never reach the shared targets. Apple checks this at every upload to App Store Connect, TestFlight's included, so the owner's first internal build already proves it.

**Stays Mac-only**: starting and watching the engine (`EngineSpawn`, `EngineTrouble`, `LoginItemKit`), the app's host roles (`ComputerKit`, `BrowserKit` and their sessions), `PaneKit`, `WindowKit`, `FileLinkKit`, `ComposeKit`, `PeerKit`, `Notifier`, Sparkle, and every view. `EngineClient` stays as the Mac's object, holding an `EngineKit` session and what only the Mac does with it.

**How the Mac app's behaviour is proven unchanged.**

1. **Moves first.** A pull request that moves code changes nothing inside what it moves but `public` and imports; the reviewer reads it with `git diff -M --color-moved`, and behaviour changes come in a later pull request.
2. **A recorded session, replayed** (`pi2-client-check`, P2): the events a throwaway copy's engine sent while `scripts/test-copy fill --project` built two demo projects and their conversations, recorded once into a fixture, are fed to the client; every property the views read is written out and compared with a file made from the client as it was before P2. P3 and P4 run it unchanged; any difference fails. Until P3, the check read each event as `EngineClient.handle` did, with a copy of its reading; since P3 it plays them to EngineKit's `EngineSession` itself ([development.md](development.md#client-checks)); CI builds it, and running it there is a change to the workflow, which is the owner's.
3. **The checks that exist** (`pi2-transcript-check`, `pi2-layout-check`, `pi2-window-check`, `pi2-compose-check`, `pi2-file-link-check`, `pi2-computer-check`) pass as before.
4. **The same pictures.** P3 and P4 picture a throwaway copy's hub, Dashboard and a long conversation before and after, in both appearances, and say what differs (nothing should).

## 9. Who it is for, and how it is given out

This section is decision 3's one place. The owner has not made it yet, and asked what changes if the App Store is planned for from the start. This page is written for what is likely: **built so that the App Store stays possible, given out to the owner alone through TestFlight's internal testing first.** The App Store is not decided by this; it stays one decision later, and [9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store) says what it would still take.

### 9.1 Given out: TestFlight's internal testing

- **The owner alone**, as an internal tester, until step 6's security reading is done and the owner says otherwise.
- **What internal testing needs from the owner**: the Apple Developer Program membership already held; the app's record in App Store Connect; test information (a short description and a feedback address); and an answer about encryption with each build, which the app's `Info.plist` gives once and for all ([9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store)).[^exportkey] Internal testers are App Store Connect users of the owner's team, up to 100; builds reach them with no review by Apple, and each build can be tested for 90 days.[^tfinternal][^tfoverview]
- **What external testers would add**: up to 10,000 people who are not on the team, invited by e-mail or a public link; the first build sent to them goes to App Review against the App Review Guidelines, and later ones may.[^tfoverview][^tfexternal] A TestFlight beta should be meant for public release and follow the guidelines, and may not be given in exchange for payment.[^beta] So external testing asks for everything of [9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store)'s review part (the demo mode, the notes for review, a privacy policy), and the relay's limits of [6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost) start to matter, since strangers' Macs use it. **No external tester before** every must-fix item of the security reading (#635) is built and P34 has passed, with RemoteKit and pi² Remote read by a reader from outside the project.
- **Identifiers** (P14): the iPhone app `dev.pi2.ios`, its notification service extension `dev.pi2.ios.notifications`, widgets `dev.pi2.ios.widgets`, share extension `dev.pi2.ios.share`; pi² Remote `dev.pi2.app.remote`. Release builds of the Mac app carry pi² Remote with its provisioning profile from P14 on.
- **Two relays** (P9): a development relay, for development builds and the sandbox push environment, and the production relay, for TestFlight builds and later. Throwaway copies use neither ([10](#10-testing-with-no-phone-in-hand)).
- **Behind a preview switch on the Mac**: Settings › Devices shows only **iPhone (preview)** until the owner turns it on (`remote.preview` in `pi2.devices`); with it off, the engine starts no helper and refuses every `devices.*` request but turning it on.
- **Builds**: a script (`scripts/iphone-release`, P14) archives and uploads to TestFlight with `xcodebuild` and Apple's own tools; no third-party build tools.

### 9.2 Nothing assumes one person

So that the App Store, or external testers, need no redesign:

- **A pairing is keys, not an account.** The Mac and each phone are known by public keys made at pairing and by random ids ([3.2](#32-the-keys)); there is no sign-in, no account of the owner's or anyone's, and no Apple Account in the protocol. Two strangers' Macs and phones are two pairings, exactly as the owner's is one.
- **The relay serves many pairings**, each in its own Durable Object with its own limits, and carries nothing of the owner's in its code: its push key, team and app ids, limits and address are its deployment's settings ([5.3](#53-the-relay), [6.4](#64-what-the-owner-sets-up-once)).
- **Only genuine copies of the app may join** (App Attest, [6.6](#66-abuse-and-cost)), and abuse is bounded per pairing, not by trusting who uses it.
- **The relay holds nothing to disclose**: channels end to end, items and pushes sealed for one reader, no logs of content ([5.3](#53-the-relay)). What it does hold (ids, public keys, push tokens, sizes and times) is the list a privacy label and policy would declare.
- **The phone's text is not the owner's**: no names, paths or projects of the owner's appear in the app; every string a person reads is written for anyone, as the Mac app's are.

### 9.3 Nothing App Review refuses on sight

- **Public APIs only**, in the phone and its extensions ([section 8](#8-the-shared-package)).[^publicapi]
- **No code downloaded**: the conversation's page ships in the app's bundle, and what comes from the Mac is data, never code the phone runs.[^selfcontained] What runs on the Mac is the Mac's; the phone runs none of it.
- **Background work only for its purposes**: remote notifications, the notification service extension, and short tasks to finish an answer; no audio, VoIP or location trick to keep a connection open.[^bgmodes]
- **IPv6-only networks work** ([5.4](#54-direct-at-home)).[^ipv6]
- **A native app, not a mirror of the Mac's screen**: every screen is drawn on the phone from the engine's data ([9.5](#95-if-it-goes-to-the-app-store) says why this matters).
- **The phone can delete what is kept about it**: **Unpair this iPhone** removes its slot, push token and mailbox at the relay and everything on the phone ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)).
- **A link to a privacy policy and a way to reach pi²** in the app's settings, from the first TestFlight build, so that nothing is added at the end.[^privacy][^devinfo]

### 9.4 The demo mode

A reviewer has no Mac running pi², and a person who has just installed the app has nothing to pair with yet. So the phone has a **demo mode**: the whole of the app against canned data, with no Mac and no network. App Review asks for a demo account, or a fully featured demo mode, with full access to the app;[^demo] pi² has no accounts, so the demo mode is how a reviewer sees everything.

- **Where**: the first screen offers **Try it without a Mac** under **Scan the code on your Mac**, and Settings offers it again until a Mac is paired. A bar across the top of every screen says **Demo**, with **Leave**.
- **How**: a transport of its own, `DemoTransport`, behind the same `EngineTransport` the channel uses ([section 8](#8-the-shared-package)), so every screen is the real one. It starts from a recorded greeting of demo projects (the same kind of recording as `pi2-client-check`'s, made with the scripted demo model, so it holds nothing of anyone's) and answers requests by script: answering a card takes it away and the agent carries on; a message to an orchestrator gets a streamed answer; a merge merges; Face ID is asked as in earnest on opening, and the attach's signatures are checked by the script.
- **What it shows**: every screen of the design, a card of every kind, a notification of every kind (scheduled locally, with their buttons), the Work tab and a pull request's diff, the Live Activity and widgets with demo numbers.
- **What it never does**: connect anywhere, register for pushes, make keys in the Secure Enclave, or write outside its own folder; leaving it deletes what it wrote.
- **Kept true**: the recording is made again by a script from the current engine whenever the greeting's shape changes, and a test plays every screen against it (P36).

### 9.5 If it goes to the App Store

**Built in already** (9.2 to 9.4): pairing by keys, not accounts; a relay for many pairings with limits for each and nothing of the owner's in it; App Attest at the relay; sealing end to end, so the relay holds no content to disclose or lose; public APIs only; the page in the bundle; IPv6; a native app; unpairing that deletes what the relay keeps; a privacy link and contact in the app; the demo mode.

## 10. Testing with no phone in hand

**A relay on this Mac.** `relay/` runs locally under Cloudflare's own local runtime (`wrangler dev`, Miniflare[^cfvitest]) on the loopback interface, with a stand-in for Apple's push service that records the pushes it is given instead of sending them. It needs no Cloudflare account and touches neither of pi²'s relays.

**The simulator against a throwaway copy's engine.**

- `scripts/iphone-sim` (P13, [development.md](development.md#building-the-iphone-app)) makes a simulator of the caller's own under a name it gives, builds, tests and runs the app there unsigned, and takes its pictures; it works on no simulator it did not make.

- `scripts/test-copy start <suffix> --remote` (P13) starts a local relay for the copy, then a copy whose engine starts pi² Remote in its **loopback mode**: it connects to that relay only, listens on 127.0.0.1 only for the `home` path, advertises nothing on Bonjour, registers for no push, and keeps its keys in a file in the copy's data folder. Loopback is not a local network, so nothing asks macOS for the Local Network privilege,[^lnp] and the copy's own rules ([CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#testing-safely)) hold: no permission prompt, nothing of the installed pi² touched. `stop` stops the local relay too. A development build's pi² Remote has no other mode.
- `scripts/test-copy pair <suffix>` opens a pairing through the copy's engine with the test copy's key and prints the `pi2://pair?…` text; `xcrun simctl openurl "$(scripts/iphone-sim id <name>)" '<url>'` hands it to the debug build in the caller's own simulator (never `booted`, which may name the person's), which pairs without scanning, over the local relay (a release build takes no pairing from a link, [3.1](#31-what-the-mac-shows-and-the-phone-scans)); the script then reads the digits the simulator's app shows, from its debug log, and sends them as the person would type them.
- The simulator has no Secure Enclave and no App Attest: a debug build of the phone makes its keys in software in the simulator, and says so on screen. It asks `targetEnvironment(simulator)`, not `SecureEnclave.isAvailable`, which answers true in the iOS 27.2 simulator on an Apple silicon Mac (`KeyPlace`, `iphone/Sources/Keys`, #680); the local relay, and only it, takes a join without an attestation when started for tests, and a throwaway copy's engine, and only such an engine, takes a pairing claim without one. A release build of the phone has no such path, and neither pi²'s relays nor a release engine take one. Face ID is the simulator's own (Features › Face ID). Pushes are tried with `xcrun simctl push` and a payload the local relay's stand-in recorded, which runs the notification service extension.
- `scripts/test-copy relay-down <suffix>` and `relay-up` stop and start the local relay, to try [6.5](#65-when-the-relay-is-down-or-the-person-wants-none).

**What CI builds and runs**:

- the relay's tests (the Workers Vitest integration, locally, on Linux): the protocol, hello and challenges, register, join with a stand-in attestation checker, a second Mac `hello` refused, builds below the minimum refused, the routing, the mailbox and its expiry (an hour for an item marked `presence`, a `"face"` request), the limits and that only authenticated traffic counts, pushes to the stand-in, revocation (P8); and that its configuration keeps logs off and its code writes nothing to the console (P15);

- App Attest's verifier against Apple's sample attestations, and against ones whose client data, app id or chain is wrong (P37);

## 11. What the Mac cannot check

The engine checks what reaches it. About a phone, pi² Remote and the relay, these lie outside, and nothing in the epic may claim otherwise:

- **That a device's keys are where they should be.** The Mac checks App Attest itself at pairing, bound to the device key and the channel ([3.3](#33-the-pairing-exchange)): a genuine pi² for iPhone made this device key. It cannot tell more: that the phone is not jailbroken, or that the key in the Secure Enclave is the only one.
- **Whose face.** A presence signature proves that the phone's enrolled Face ID passed. It cannot prove whose face is enrolled, or that the passcode that guards enrolling is strong.
- **What the phone keeps, and shows.** The Mac cannot see the phone's cache, its screenshots, its delivered notifications, its Lock Screen setting, or whether it is jailbroken.
- **Who holds a phone while pi² is open.** Face ID opens a session and nothing is asked after ([4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice), the owner's choice). Whoever takes the phone from the person's hand while pi² is open, or within a minute of leaving it, can do everything the table allows, merges and widening answers included, until the session ends; the Mac notifies the person of each widening request, and keeps Allow everything to itself. Unlocked without the owner's face, the phone opens no session. A thief who watched the passcode and can enrol a face gets nothing more until the person accepts the new Face ID key on the Mac. **Revoking needs the Mac**: away from it, the remedy is Find My's Erase iPhone.
- **What pi² Remote does once a device is attached.** The engine takes from the helper it started any request the device's table allows, as the device ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)). The engine checks every attach and every resume itself, with both signatures, but it trusts its own child to pass the device's lines faithfully. That is why the helper is the engine's own child, steered by nothing else.
- **An agent that goes round pi² on purpose.** A command that rewrites the data folder (`engine.pid`, the database) or stops the engine and starts one of its own is outside these checks, as [projects.md 7.3](projects.md#73-who-answered) says. Here it would also open a network door, which pi² Remote's own list of device keys keeps shut: a device written into the database is not attached.
- **The engine cannot tell what a shell command does.** An approval to run `gh pr merge` is an ordinary approval, not a merge; the notification the Mac posts for a widening request ([4.4](#44-the-devices-log)) covers merges made through pi²'s own switches and requests, as [projects.md 11](projects.md#11-what-the-engine-cannot-check) says of merging. Every approval from a phone is made in a session Face ID opened.
- **What the relay does with what it sees.** The channel and every sealed item are end to end, so the relay cannot read or change them unnoticed, nor replay or reorder inside a channel, nor join two devices that are not a pair. It can drop, delay or hold a whole channel; hold or replay mailbox items, which the nonces and the one-hour and 24-hour limits bound; and it learns sizes and times. It can delay **Stop**: when an agent runs away, **Stop on the Mac is the one to rely on**. It can flood the Mac's socket, which pi² Remote bounds by limiting handshakes and frames per device. The same holds for Cloudflare.
- **That a push arrived.** Apple's push service is best effort;[^apns] a need answered nowhere stays under Needs you on the Mac, as today.
- **When something was written.** `at` in a sealed item is the phone's clock; the engine bounds it with its own (24 hours, one hour for a `"face"` request, and five minutes of difference besides).
- **What [projects.md section 11](projects.md#11-what-the-engine-cannot-check) already says** stays true: an agent's command can still write the data folder or start an engine of its own; the device rules do not sandbox commands.

**What each key gives to whoever copies it**, with the changes of the security reading in place:

| Key | Kept | What a copy gives |
|---|---|---|
| Phone device key | Secure Enclave | Cannot be copied; usable only on that phone, unlocked |
| Phone presence key | Secure Enclave, Face ID only | Cannot be copied; signs only under a live Face ID |
| Phone channel key | Keychain | A handshake, but no attach or resume |
| Phone record key | Keychain, after first unlock | Reads that phone's pushes and mailbox items; cannot forge one from the Mac, which signs them |
| Mac identity key | Secure Enclave (where there is one) | Cannot be copied |
| Mac channel key | Keychain (group) | A handshake as the Mac, but phones also check the identity key's signature |
| Mac record key | Keychain (group), or Secure Enclave | Reads items waiting for the Mac (24 hours); cannot forge a phone's, which are signed |
| Mac relay key | Secure Enclave (where there is one) | Takes the pairing at the relay: delete it, see its traffic's sizes |
| APNs key | The relay's secrets | Pushes to pi² phones whose tokens are known; the notification service extension shows only text from inside a verified seal, so it can say nothing but *pi²: something needs you* |

### 12.3 Before anything is built: the repository (P0)

pi² is built by agents that merge their own pull requests once CI passes (`scripts/auto-merge`), and that read issues and comments anyone on GitHub can write. A worker talked into it by a planted comment could move a request in the device lists from Face ID to yes, weaken a check in RemoteKit, or make the relay keep addresses or post its push key, and it would be merged and deployed. And anyone with write access to a repository can read its secrets, through a workflow pushed on any branch.[^ghsecrets] So before any issue of the relay, the channel, the helper or the device lists (P5, P6, P7, P8 and P37 wait for P0, and so does every issue that changes one of the paths below), the repository needs:

- **The agents' own identity on GitHub.** On the owner's Mac the agents use the owner's own sign-in (`gh`, the `work` tool, every push), so every pull request an agent opens is the owner's, and GitHub does not let the author of a pull request approve it:[^ghauthor] the owner's review could never be given, and the rule could only be got round. And whatever the owner's sign-in may do, an agent holding it may do: change branch protection, approve a deployment, merge past a review; branch protection does not apply to administrators unless told to.[^ghadmins] So the agents get an account of their own, with a collaborator's rights, which on a personal repository are to push, open, review and merge pull requests and keep issues, and include no setting, protection, environment or secret;[^ghcollab] the owner's own sign-in is taken out of `gh`, git and every token on the Mac and used in a browser alone, which no agent is given. Two ways, both written out in [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#1-the-agents-own-identity-on-github): a machine account (recommended: it works with `gh` and the `work` tool as they are) or a GitHub App (narrower permissions, but a program on the Mac to make its hourly tokens). Issues, comments and pull requests are then the agents' account's; a release's tag too, while the owner approves the release run and publishes it. What it does not cover: agents run as the owner's macOS user, so any session of the owner's on GitHub or Cloudflare in any browser profile of that user, a leftover token or an SSH key is within reach of an agent's commands, whatever tool it is denied; the owner does that administration from another device or macOS user ([`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#1-the-agents-own-identity-on-github)). The release, what the protected workflows run, and `pull_request_target` are left open, in [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#what-this-does-not-cover).
- **`CODEOWNERS` naming the owner** for `relay/`, `app/Sources/RemoteKit/`, `app/Sources/pi2-remote/`, `engine/src/devices.ts`, `engine/src/remote.ts`, `engine/src/appattest.ts`, `engine/src/answers.ts` (which holds the device lists), and, once P14 and the extensions' issues add them, the iPhone app's entitlements and signing and upload configuration. **And for the guard's own files**: `.github/CODEOWNERS`, `scripts/auto-merge`, `scripts/test-auto-merge`, `.github/workflows/` as a whole (`relay.yml` and `iphone.yml` among them), and `docs/relay.md`, the owner's own instructions, which an edit could make mislead, since any workflow can be changed to name a protected environment, and one run from `main` uses it once approved. The cost: every later change to `scripts/auto-merge`, its test or any workflow waits for the owner too.
- **Branch protection on `main` requiring the code owner's review** for those paths, with no approvals required for the rest, so that the agents' other pull requests still merge by themselves; stale approvals dismissed; and no bypassing, for administrators too (a repository setting, the owner's to make: [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#2-branch-protection-on-main) has the boxes).
- **`scripts/auto-merge` refusing** a pull request that touches any of those paths, and saying it waits for the owner's review; `scripts/test-auto-merge` checks it. It reads `CODEOWNERS` from GitHub, not from the checkout: as it is on `main`, which a pull request cannot change, and at the pull request's newest commit, which can only add to it. Main's copy missing or unreadable, a line it cannot read exactly as GitHub does, or a head commit this repository cannot read, refuses; and every merge it makes is bound to the commit it checked. It is a courtesy to the agents, not the guard; the guard is the branch protection.
- **Two GitHub environments, `relay-development` and `relay-production`**, each limited to `main`, with the owner as required reviewer; the Cloudflare token, scoped to Workers on an account that holds only the relay, is a secret of those environments alone, never of the repository.[^ghenv] The relay's workflow deploys only through them.
- **The APNs key never in GitHub**: topic-specific for `dev.pi2.ios`, one per environment, put in the relay's secrets by the owner with `wrangler secret put` ([6.4](#64-what-the-owner-sets-up-once)).
- **The iPhone app's signing and upload credentials** (P14) in a protected environment too, never in reach of a workflow run from a branch.
- **The `release` environment with the owner as required reviewer**: it holds the Mac app's signing keys, and the agents' own account can push the `v*` tags that start the release workflow.

An agent writes `CODEOWNERS`, the change to `scripts/auto-merge` and the list in [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#the-owners-settings-before-anything-is-built); **the owner makes the settings**, which no agent may change. This page changes none of them. **P0 (#668) is done when its files are merged.** By the owner's decision (34), the agents' account and the settings are deferred to [#712](https://github.com/KellamA/pi2/issues/712), which also holds a second agent's full security read of the owner's paths and the four checks of [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#checking-the-settings). The first real pairing of a phone over the relay (P20's real push) and the production relay's deploy (P9) wait for it. Until then the rule is kept by convention: `scripts/auto-merge` refuses, a second agent reads each protected change for security, and the owner merges it. That stops an accident; it does not stop an agent talked into merging around the script, since the agents hold the owner's sign-in.

## 13. Decisions this contract made

1. **One table decides what a device may send** ([4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send)), checked on every request of a device connection whatever its class, and on every sealed item; every handler is placed in it, or the engine will not start.
2. **Face ID is a signature, not a flag**: the key that signs works only under the Face ID that opened the session, so the engine checks signatures on every attach, resume and mailbox item, fresh and used once.
3. **One channel over two paths**: the relay is the path that must always work; direct is an optimisation with the same keys, handshake and messages, chosen when the two can see each other, and a session moves between them.
4. **Noise, not TLS**, because the channel must run inside a WebSocket as well as over TCP, either end in either role, between ends that already know each other's keys; implemented once, in RemoteKit, against the published test vectors.
5. **The device key in the Secure Enclave binds the channel key, which cannot be there**, by signing the handshake hash at every connection.
6. **pi² Remote holds both paths, the keys and the channels**, started by the engine and living as long as it; the engine stays the judge ([5.5](#55-which-program-holds-them)).
7. **The engine's own requests and events are carried as they are**, filtered in the engine both ways.
8. **The relay can read nothing**: channels are end to end, mailbox items and pushes are sealed with HPKE for one reader, no category or kind is visible on a push, and the relay logs nothing of them; the one exception is a Live Activity's numbers, which the person turns on.
9. **The relay serves only genuine pi² for iPhone**: a pairing is pending until a phone with an App Attest attestation and the QR code's token joins it.
10. **The relay keeps a small mailbox**, 24 hours, so that an answer written while the Mac sleeps, or a need while the phone is away, is not lost.
11. **Pushes come from the relay**, with pi²'s push key, not from iCloud.
12. **Pairing only on the Mac**, with a five-minute code and six digits worked out from the channel, confirmed by a click in the app; on any network.
13. **A new Face ID key must be accepted on the Mac.**
14. **Every notification button opens pi²**, which asks Face ID if no session is open and shows the whole card before acting.
15. **A notification says who, what kind, which project and the card's short line**, secrets replaced, all inside the seal.
16. **The phone's cache is small, protected and wiped.**
17. **Moves before changes** in the shared package, proven by a recorded session replayed against the client.
18. **Throwaway copies use a relay on the same Mac and listen on loopback only**, so testing asks macOS for nothing and touches neither of pi²'s relays.
19. **Behind a preview switch on the Mac**, as projects were, until the owner says otherwise.
20. **Built for the App Store, given out by TestFlight**: pairings are keys, not accounts; the relay serves many pairings with nothing of the owner's in it; public APIs only, the page in the bundle, IPv6; [9.2](#92-nothing-assumes-one-person) and [9.3](#93-nothing-app-review-refuses-on-sight).
21. **A demo mode through its own transport**, so a reviewer, and a person before pairing, see every real screen against canned data with no Mac ([9.4](#94-the-demo-mode)).
22. **The phone can unpair itself**, which deletes what the relay keeps of it, with or without the Mac ([3.6](#36-revoking-and-a-lost-phone)).
23. **The relay's `hello` has room for a proof of purchase** (`ext`), unused, so that charging for the relay later needs no new protocol.

**After the security reading (#635, answered in #651):**

24. **pi² Remote is trusted as the engine's own child, not by its signature** (M1): the engine takes devices' lines and `remote.*` requests only on the socket pair it made for the helper it started; a copy not started by the engine exits before reading a key; a release build takes no settings from outside; the relay refuses a second Mac socket. The one gap left is an agent that rewrites the data folder, which [projects.md 7.3](projects.md#73-who-answered) already leaves out, and which pi² Remote's own list of device keys narrows.
25. **Pairing takes the digits typed on the Mac, one claim per window, App Attest checked by the Mac on both paths, and `s` and claims only on the app's connection; no pairing from a link** (M2).
26. **Face ID opens the app, nothing after: the owner's choice** (M3, 21 November 2026, [4.5](#45-a-stolen-or-unlocked-phone-the-owners-choice)). Biometrics only; a session ends after 60 seconds out of the foreground, on lock and on restart; a notification's action counts as opening. **Not taken: the reading's recommendation**, Face ID again for the risky answers inside a session. What that leaves open: a phone taken while pi² is open can merge, widen and approve with one tap until the session ends. What this page does about it within the owner's choice: the Mac notifies the person of each widening request from a device, and keeps Allow everything on to itself pending question 5.
27. **What a phone may send is decided by what a request does** (M4); under decision 26 the question is whether a phone may send a request at all, not whether it asks Face ID again. The requests the reading named were each checked in the engine: `chat.set`, `conversation.fork`, the unarchive and restore requests, `github.settle` (with `happened: false` it lets a write happen again; the card says so) and approving an agent's merge or release may all be sent in an open session, as the person may do them at the Mac. `projects.watch` attaches nothing (it chooses which tabs a connection watches), so it is a reading request, and P5 corrects its comment in `answers.ts`. `agents.allowEverything` on is kept to the Mac, pending question 5. The widening ones live an hour in the mailbox and are notified on the Mac. `work.act` stays "yes", for the reason in [4.2](#42-what-a-device-may-send).
28. **A resume is a new attach**, signed with the device key and the presence key over the new channel and a challenge from the engine (M5); pi² Remote decides nothing of it alone, and the presence key ties every new channel to the Face ID that opened the session.
29. **The repository before the code** (M6, P0): the owner's review for the paths that decide who is the person, deploy secrets only in protected environments, a topic-specific APNs key never in GitHub.
30. **The Mac has an identity key in its Secure Enclave**, and everything it writes is signed (S1); revocations the phone acts on are the Mac's (S2).
31. **The phone's cache is wiped after 7 days without the Mac**, not 30, and kept out of backups (S4).
32. **The home listener takes only local and private-network addresses unless the person turns Direct from any address on**, and Bonjour no longer carries the pairing's id (S6).

**After the owner's answers (2026-11-21):**

33. **This repository holds the code and is the source of truth for the issues**: the iPhone app (`iphone/`), the relay (`relay/`) and the shared package (`app/Package.swift`'s targets, RemoteKit among them) live here beside the engine and this contract, and every issue of the epic is filed and kept here, wherever any of its code ends up.

**After the repository's preparation (P0, #668):**

34. **The agents get a GitHub identity of their own, and the owner's sign-in comes off the Mac, before the first real phone is paired** ([12.3](#123-before-anything-is-built-the-repository-p0)): without it the owner's review cannot be given to an agent's pull request, and every rule the owner may get round, the agents may. **Decided by the owner, 2026-11-21: deferred until the first real pairing**, as [#712](https://github.com/KellamA/pi2/issues/712), with GitHub's settings. Until then the rule is kept by convention: `scripts/auto-merge` refuses a pull request touching the owner's paths, a second agent reads each such change for security, and the owner merges it. That stops an agent's mistake and an agent that follows the script; it does not stop an agent talked into merging around it, since the agents hold the owner's sign-in. Nothing is exposed meanwhile, since no phone is paired with an installed build. When the account is made, it is a machine account, as [`docs/relay.md`](relay.md#1-the-agents-own-identity-on-github) recommends, unless the owner chooses otherwise then. The guard's own files (`CODEOWNERS`, `scripts/auto-merge` and its test, every workflow) are the owner's paths too.

10. **Answered by the owner, 2026-11-21**: "Face ID to open the app, nothing after." With it, a session ends after **60 seconds** out of the foreground, on lock and on restart, and Face ID allows **no passcode fallback**? **Answered yes by the owner, 2026-11-21:** a minute is a banking app's habit, and the passcode is what a thief who watched it has.
11. **Should a new Face ID key work only once accepted on the Mac?** **Answered yes by the owner, 2026-11-21:** otherwise whoever knows the passcode could enrol a face and open pi².

Decision 3 (TestFlight, the owner alone, first): the security reading supports it strongly. Keep it so until the must-fix items are built and P34 has passed.
