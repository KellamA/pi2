# A worker's question that does not hold it

*Kept as history: this is the design as it was made, not the app as it is. For the app as it is, see [the guide](../guide.md); for projects' exact names and shapes, [the contract](../projects.md).*

A design for #507. **Agreed and built:** the owner agreed it on 2026-11-20, as proposed in #570, with all five suggested answers below, and #507 built it. What it recommends is now the contract's text in [projects.md](../projects.md), in the sections it touches, each marked "(#507)", and decision 32 in its section 13 records the answers. The rest of this page is the proposal as the owner read it.

## The gap

The design says what an agent puts under Needs you "does not stop the agent that asked from doing other work" ([projects.md](projects.md#needs-you)), and the contract builds that for the orchestrator's and agents' own conversations: `needs_you` returns at once and the answer comes back later as a message. A worker — a sub-chat — is refused the tool, because a sub-chat is never woken and an answer could not reach it once its run has ended, so its only way to ask is `ask_user`, which holds the run until the person answers ([projects.md](../projects.md) 5.5). A worker building the Needs you card for this project's own hub might need the person to decide whether a decision's buttons read as they do now or carry the choice's words in them, while the rest of the card does not depend on it. With the person away for the evening, the worker sits held for hours over one line of text — or guesses, and the person comes back to wording they were never asked about.

## Three ways it could work

**(a) The worker asks in its own name.** The person sees a card under Needs you like any other, naming the agent that asks ("Forge, in sub-chat 14 “Fix login”") and, when the run was assigned one, the piece of work; answered in place, wherever the list shows. The worker carries on meanwhile. The answer reaches it as a wake of the kind `answer`, queued while its run goes and given as its next input, within the wakes' bounds; if the run has ended, the answer goes instead to the conversation that handed it the work — for the orchestrator, its current one — with whose question it was, the sub-chat, the piece of work and how to continue it (`delegate` with `chat`), and the worker itself is not restarted. It costs a moderate change in the two places the contract already names: `needs.ts` must offer `needs_you` in a sub-chat and route the answer (the pieces exist — `pi2.needs` stores the question across a restart, `claim` is the one way an answer is taken, the `answer` wake already exists), and a sub-chat must accept an arrived wake, which is a routing decision more than a mechanism. The card gains its line, and the catch-up gains one row of news.

**(b) The question goes to the orchestrator first.** The worker puts its question to its orchestrator as a message, which answers it itself if it can and only then, with its own `needs_you`, puts it to the person. The person sees either nothing (the orchestrator answered) or a card in the orchestrator's name, reworded by the one agent that did not ask it. The worker carries on meanwhile, and the answer comes back as a message continuing the sub-chat, which costs a new run of it. It costs almost nothing to build, but every question costs a full orchestrator turn before it even reaches the person, waking it for what it usually cannot answer; a question waits on the orchestrator's wake settings, as a report does; and the person can no longer tell which agent is waiting on what, which the design put in one place precisely so they could.

**(c) The question waits for the report.** The worker finishes what it can, ends its run with the question in its report, and the orchestrator puts it to the person with `needs_you` and, once answered, carries the sub-chat on with the answer. The person sees the question only after the worker has stopped, as the orchestrator's card. The worker meanwhile does nothing: it has ended, and delivering the answer costs a new run of it. It costs nothing in the engine beyond a line in the `project` section, but it keeps the very stop being designed away, and the person's answer is twice removed from the agent that asked.

## Recommendation

(a). It is the issue's own sketch, and the design's own words made true: the person answers the agent that asked, by name, and the worker carries on. Every engine piece it needs is already there except the routing — a question is stored, answered once through `claim`, and delivered as a wake of kind `answer`; the ended case needs only the route `answerGoesTo` already takes for an earlier conversation of the orchestrator. (b) puts the person's card behind the orchestrator's judgement of what is worth asking and wakes the orchestrator for questions it cannot answer; (c) stops the worker and hides who asked.

Left out deliberately: approvals, reach requests and rule proposals stay as they are — an approval gates the worker's very next action, and reach and rules are the orchestrator's own tools (5.1); a worker outside a project keeps `ask_user`; the orchestrator cannot answer a worker's question in the person's place, and the person cannot be asked on the worker's behalf by anyone but the worker; the wakes' bounds (the budget, the hourly cap, the person's Stop) hold a worker's answer-wake as any other.

## What it changes

- **The contract** ([projects.md](../projects.md)): 3.9 (where the answer goes, and the card's line); 5.1 (the row: a sub-chat is offered `needs_you`); 5.5 (the bullet that refuses it); 5.8 (how a worker is told `ask_user` apart from `needs_you`); 6.4 (one row of news); 14, question 8 (the recommendation). Each passage was marked **Proposed (#507, not agreed)** until the owner agreed it. Nothing in 3.10 changes: whether the run has ended is read from the report `settle` keeps, not changed by it.
- **The engine**: `needs.ts` (offer `needs_you` in a sub-chat; store the piece of work with the need; route the answer — the sub-chat while its run goes, the conversation that handed it the work once ended); `wakes.ts` (a sub-chat may have an arrived wake of the kind `answer`); `orchestrator.ts` (`answerGoesTo` for a sub-chat whose run has ended; the news line); the `project` section (a worker is told which tool to use when). `reports.ts` and `settle` untouched.
- **The app**: the one card in its three places — the hub, the Dashboard and the menu bar — gains its line: which agent asks, in which sub-chat, on which piece of work. No other screen.
- **Stays as it is**: `ask_user`, for a question that truly cannot wait — when the worker's next step needs the answer and there is no other work to do meanwhile, so holding the run is the honest thing and it would otherwise end with nothing else done. An approval always holds, whatever asked it.

## Questions for the owner

1. Is (a) the way: the worker's question under Needs you in its own name, answered to the worker while it works and to the conversation that handed it the work once it has ended? *Suggested: yes.*
2. When the worker's run has ended, does the answer wake that conversation at once, or wait as one line in its next catch-up? *Suggested: wake it at once — the person has just acted, as with an answer to the orchestrator's own question.*
3. Does the card name the piece of work when the run was assigned one, or only the agent and its sub-chat? *Suggested: name the work too.*
4. Is this for a sub-chat of any project agent, or only of the orchestrator? *Suggested: any — the answer's route is the same either way.*
5. Does `ask_user` stay offered to a worker in a project, for what cannot wait, or does everything go under Needs you? *Suggested: it stays.*
